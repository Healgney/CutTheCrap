# RQ notes -- Agent C (clusters 4 and 5). Tags: [F] confirmed in primary source, [I] my inference, [V] to verify.

## RQ1 -- Has anyone defined and measured self-containedness of a clip/excerpt?

### Answer to (a): partial precedents, no direct measure
[F] In video/audio, no paper found that defines a clip-level self-containedness measure and validates it. Queries in search_log #27-31 (arXiv API, WebSearch extended x3, OpenAlex). Only prose-level requirements and ad hoc human items exist. [I] Absence evidence is only as good as these queries; terms like "completeness", "narrative integrity", "context-free", "trailer/teaser quality" could hide a paper; recommend a second pass by the cluster-1 agent.

Nearest neighbours (1-3) and the specific difference:
1. **C03 Spotify podcast previews (ACL 2025 Industry, 2505.23908)** [F]. Selects one ~1-min contiguous sentence-aligned span whose stated goal is a "self-contained moment easy to understand without additional context"; evaluated by pairwise human preference plus Yes/No ratings of understandability / contextual clarity / interest (238 valid annotations, ~20 internal raters; LLM >= legacy 81.09%) and an A/B test. Differences from us: (i) self-containedness is enforced by prompt and only judged as one binary human item, not defined, decomposed (dangling reference / missing setup / unanswered question / punchline without setup) or validated against an independent measure; (ii) one preview per episode, no K-ranking; (iii) no engagement-label supervision; (iv) no boundary-precision evaluation; (v) text-only, transcript sentences. The idea "pick self-contained spans" is therefore NOT novel; a validated, decomposed, automatically computable score and its interaction with engagement is the open piece [I].
2. **C15 Bridging Context Gaps (COLING 2025, 2412.19966)** [F]. Measures how much human-selected excerpts from ~66-min multi-party conversations lose outside the conversation (Likert understandability original 3.43 vs 4.13 after LLM context-enrichment; completeness 3.07 -> 3.83; 75 raters; HSE 1,774 excerpts released). Difference: repair-by-adding-context rather than selection; text only; no formal metric; no engagement; 90 excerpts evaluated.
3. **C14 Decontextualization (TACL 2021, 2102.05169)** [F]. Formal definition ("interpretable in the empty context" with same truth-conditional meaning), 3-way label (stand-alone / feasible / infeasible), 11,290+1,945+1,945 Wikipedia sentences; only 20-31% of sentences stand alone as-is. Difference: sentence-level written Wikipedia, editing not selecting; kappa 0.30 (crowd) / 0.51 (expert).
Also close: **C24 PodReels** [F] (self-contained is a creator-derived design principle; human-in-loop context UI, no metric), **C25 TruNet** [F] (coherent "integral story" units scored only via IoU), **C17 ExtEval** [F] (dangling coreference/discourse in extracted summaries, detector r=0.48/0.61).

### What exists to build a self-containedness score, with validation numbers (b)
- Coreference / dangling-reference detectors. [F] ExtEval IncomCorefEval r=0.48, IncomDiscoEval r=0.61 vs human labels on 1,600 CNN/DM extractive summaries (C17); incomplete coref 15.4% and incomplete discourse 10.7% of extractive summaries. [F] Maverick CoNLL-F1 up to 83.6 on written OntoNotes with 504M params (C22). [F] On spoken multi-party dialogue, coref drops sharply: CODI-CRAC 2021 final avg F1 AMI 63.96, LIGHT 80.33, PSUA 78.41, SWBD 74.49, with the baseline mention-ranking system at 31.7 on AMI (C23) [gold transcripts; ASR effect not measured]. [I] Expect pronoun-antecedent checks on ASR podcast text to be noisier than news; needs validation on our own annotated clips.
- LLM judge for omission-type coherence errors. [F] BooookScore (C19): GPT-4 judge precision 78.2% vs 79.7% for humans on 1,659 judged annotations; system-level scores within 0.3-1.4 pts; no kappa, recall unmeasurable; eight error types incl. entity / event / causal omission. [F] SNaC (C18): crowd segment-level alpha 0.69 (expert 0.90), but RefE (missing reference to event/object) span alpha 0.25 expert / 0.10 crowd; trained classifier F1 CharE .80 vs RefE .21-.32. [I] "Missing setup of a person/entity" is reliably detectable; "missing earlier event" is not -- risk for a "needs prior context" label.
- Decontextualization models as a proxy. [F] T5-11B feasibility accuracy 89% vs 88% always-feasible baseline (C14); correct decontextualization 67.5%; QaDecontext only 38% convincing, gold rewrites acceptable 42% (C16). [I] Rewrite-based scoring is too weak to be a clip score; judge-style or classifier-style scoring is more promising.
- Selection-time dependency constraints. [F] Durrett et al. 2016 (C21, abstract-only): include antecedent or replace pronoun with full NP. [F] Referential-dangling preprint (C20): relevance-only selection leaves 34-60% of examples with dangling support; re-inserting the missing paragraph gives +28.8 to +34.3 pts QA accuracy; a BERT-base "needed-to-interpret" ranker +4.7 pts (preprint, Aug 2026, [V]-grade).
- Human-process precedents. [F] TREC 2020 summary rubric: Excellent requires "coherent", "standalone" snippet; attribute questions "sentence" (starts/ends at sentence boundaries) and "title-context" (C01). [F] Spotify previews: Yes/No understandability and contextual clarity (C03).

### (c) TREC Podcasts task definition and evaluation (summary)
[F] 2020 (C01): ~100k English episodes with Google ASR transcripts (0.1 s word timestamps), audio and creator metadata. Task 1: retrieve 2-min segments (start on minute, 1-min overlap, ~3.4M segments) for topic queries; NIST graded PEGFB; mean nDCG (also nDCG@30, P@10); best 0.67 vs BM25 0.52; transcript 0.58 vs title-only 0.22. Task 2: short, grammatical, standalone text summary; NIST EGFB (4-2-1-0) + 8 yes/no attributes; ROUGE-L vs creator descriptions correlates only r=0.28 with EGFB; best EGFB 2.04 vs creator descriptions 1.45; audio unused by participants. [F] 2021 (C02, task page only): added non-topical targets (entertaining, opinionated, contains discussion), assessors use audio as well as transcript, summarization also asks for an audio clip giving the sound of the episode; participant-level audio gains and the 2021 overview PDF not read [V]. [I] Segment unit is fixed (no boundary choice) and relevance is query-based, so TREC does not test self-containedness or boundary precision.

## RQ5 -- Gains from audio / laughter / prosody over transcript-only (only with ablation)
- [F] Rhapsody (C04), podcast highlights from Most-Replayed: FT Llama-3.2-1B hit rate text-only 0.452 -> text+HuBERT 0.490 (+3.8 pts); Gemini text+audio not consistently better than text-only; GPT-4o text-only 0.265 ~ random 0.220. Single run, [V] seeds/CI. Audio vector is time-averaged HuBERT/DVA, not laughter-specific.
- [F] Humor/laughter: sitcom Friends text 62% -> text+audio 78% acc (+16, C11); FunnyNet-W Friends F1 T 77.8 -> A+T 84.5 -> V+A+T 88.2 (C12); TED (UR-FUNNY) text 64.44 vs +acoustic 64.47 vs all 65.23 (C10). [I] Large in laugh-track sitcoms (audio leaks audience/prosodic cues), near zero in TED lectures; no podcast evidence.
- [F] TruNet (C25): audio-only 51.0 vs RGB-only 41.33 mAP, both 57.34 (generic CNN audio, TV/variety shows; not speech/laughter specific).
- [F] Spotify previews (C03): a text-only LLM beat a legacy system that used audio signals (language ID, sound events, sentence-break detection, ad detection) -- not an ablation; [I] audio helped little relative to better text reasoning in that production setting.
- [F, abstract-only] Topic segmentation: audio embeddings can be replaced by sentence duration and inter-sentence pause features with comparable results (C07); podcast extractive summarization improves with audio encodings (C08); speech summarization: acoustic/prosodic + structural features suffice without transcripts, all features best (C09). Numbers not read.
- [I] Bottom line for RQ5: documented, quantified audio gains exist but are (i) outside podcasts, (ii) mostly on laugh-labeled data where leakage is possible, or (iii) abstract-only. For podcasts the only controlled number is Rhapsody's +3.8 pts hit rate. No paper found that ablates laughter/applause/prosody specifically for clip selection from podcasts (search_log #14-18).

# Addendum after deep reading (details in deep/*.md). Tags: [F] primary source read, [I] inference, [V] unverified.

## RQ1 final conclusion
- [F] After 36 logged queries and full reads of C03/C14/C15/C17/C16/C19/C20/C25 (and C04 for RQ5), I still find no work that defines clip-level self-containedness for audio/video and validates a score against human judgments on spoken clips. Nearest neighbours are unchanged in rank: (1) C03 Spotify previews, (2) C15 Bridging Context Gaps, (3) C14 Decontextualization. One adjacent seed paper deserves a place next to them: Repurpose-10K (Wu et al., AAAI 2025; cluster 1): clips are described as "self-contained sub-topic" units and its human study (10 raters, 462 clips per group) scores "completeness level" 1-5 next to engagement: random 1.508, professional editors 3.738, dataset annotations 3.418 (own grep of the HTML; the construct is not defined in the lines found and no agreement is reported) [F for numbers, V for definition]. This shows "completeness" is rated by humans in the repurposing literature but never decomposed or modeled; it does not change the RQ1 novelty conclusion, but Agent A should read its human-study section.
- [F] What C03 really measured (corrected): its "understandability" item asks whether the preview helps decide relevance (not whether it is interpretable alone); its "contextual clarity" item asks whether preview plus episode metadata gives enough context. Both are binary, ~20 internal raters, 238 valid annotations, no agreement number, no per-question percentages (only z statistics -4.05, -3.40, -4.32), and the pairwise win/tie/loss counts are not given (81.09% = win or tie; 54.2% win).
- [F] Text-side measurability evidence: Decontextualization kappa 0.51 expert / 0.30 crowd; QaDecontext 70% binary agreement between two coauthors and only 42% of gold rewrites judged acceptable; SNaC reference-type spans alpha 0.10-0.25; Bridging Context Gaps and BooookScore report no agreement at all. [I] A human self-containedness label for clips will need multiple raters, an explicit rubric, and reported agreement; expect modest kappa.

## (b) Can the C17 and C20 detectors be moved to ASR transcripts? (only numbers read in the papers)
- C17 ExtEval: [F] correlations (IncomCorefEval r=0.48; IncomDiscoEval r=0.61; incorrect-coref precision 87.5% / recall 46.7%) are measured on 1,600 CNN/DM extractive summaries, punctuated edited news, full source available, closed word lists made from CNN/DM, SpanBERT clusters from AllenNLP 2.4.0; no speech, ASR or dialogue test. [F] Spoken-dialogue coreference is much weaker than news (C23: AMI 63.96 avg F1 vs OntoNotes ~83-84 CoNLL F1 for C22, gold transcripts). [I] The decision rule (first mention of a clip entity is a pronoun/determiner-NP while the episode transcript introduces it earlier) transfers conceptually; the published correlations do not. Needs punctuation restoration/sentence segmentation, a spoken discourse-marker list, and validation on our own labelled clips.
- C20 Referential dangling: [F] numbers are on written QA corpora with annotated support paragraphs; the detector is a content-word coverage heuristic (theta 0.5) needing support annotations, plus a first-mention diagnostic for unannotated documents (mean 30.5% of sentences' references dangle on LongBench-v2); the only human check is 38/40 flagged examples confirmed; the restoration classifier gains +4.7 on HotpotQA but +0.5 on 2Wiki and is non-significant on other compressors; preprint, not peer reviewed. [I] Treat as motivation (relevance-only selection leaves referents behind, large downstream cost when repaired) and as a design template, not as a validated detector for ASR.
- [I] Bottom line: neither paper gives a number that predicts detector quality on podcast ASR; a small pilot (annotate ~200 clips, compare ExtEval-style coref/discourse flags and an LLM judge) is needed before claiming a self-containedness score works on speech.

## (c) Spotify preview human evaluation, as verified in the full text
- [F] Questions (Yes/No per preview): Q1 "Does the preview help you decide if this episode is relevant for you?"; Q2 "Does the preview plus metadata contain enough context to understand what is being talked about from the preview?"; Q3 "Does the preview show an interesting part of the episode?"; pairwise "Which preview is better?" with a tie option; raters saw title, description and show name, listened with subtitles, order randomized; about 20 internal raters, about 20 episodes each (unique count unstated), 238 valid annotations; no agreement statistic; z-tests -4.05 / -3.40 / -4.32 with p 5.09e-5 / 6.7e-4 / 1.59e-5 but test specification unclear; 81.09% win-or-tie and 54.2% win (binomial p 1.37e-10); A/B 6 weeks, 67 English-speaking countries, 34% of episodes covered, +4.6% evaluation time per user and +4% per preview in week 2 (abstract calls it engagement; no sample size or p-value). No limitations section.
