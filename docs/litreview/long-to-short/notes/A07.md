## Label source: reuse in short-form content (question a)
- [F] Collect ~5,000 high-engagement short videos derived from movies/TV dramas from short-video platforms; an LLM labels clips from titles and filters irrelevant ones; valid clips split into 2-s non-overlapping clips.
- [F] Training-set labels: "frequent reuse of video clips across user-generated content as an implicit indicator of highlight value", traced back to the source videos with video fingerprinting; occurrence frequencies aggregated into a per-clip voting score. Final training set = 1,368 videos with high-scoring, "crowd-validated" clips (used only as video-level labels in stage 1 MIL; clip-level labels are LLM pseudo-labels).
- [F] Test set: 353 videos; multiple human annotators label highlights at 2-s intervals; majority vote; 5 semantic categories (action, destruction, CGI, emotional climax, others). Total 1,721 videos in 15 genres. Authors "will release" dataset + annotations (not verified).
- [F] The table of datasets marks Movie Highlight (ref [11]) as human-annotated: No, publicly available: Forthcoming; TVHighlights as not query-guided.

## Method and results
- [F] LTV-HD: LTRM (local-attention multimodal transformer with CLIP ViT-L-14 + SlowFast visual, ImageBind audio); stage 1 top-K MIL with video-level labels (K=6); stage 2 iterative collaboration: MiniCPM-V 2.6 descriptions + DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen produce noisy clip pseudo-labels, noisy-label-cleaning loss, highlight-pattern induction fed back to the LLM (2 rounds).
- [F] Table 2 total: LTV-HD Stage 2 AUC 92.74 / AP 71.20; Stage 1 AUC 87.34 / AP 62.39 (text: +5.40 AUC, +8.81 AP from stage 1 to 2); best MLLM Qwen-vl-max per-clip scoring AUC 88.40 / AP 47.42; Qwen-vl-max moment-retrieval AP 36.62; per-clip scoring beats direct moment retrieval consistently ("MLLMs struggle with precise temporal localization").
- [F] Emotion category AP 60.00 vs 41.38 best MLLM; most baselines do better on visually salient Action/Destruction than Emotional Climax.
- [F] YouTube Highlights clean average mAP 75.8 (UVCOM2 77.4 higher). Label-quality: Cohen's kappa of generated labels vs human rises across rounds (Table 7; exact values not extracted).
- [F] No modality (audio vs visual) ablation in the main paper; audio is just an input stream. Code availability and licence not stated [V].
- [I] Takeaways for us: (1) "downstream reuse count" is a usable weak label that needs a fingerprinting step, (2) per-clip scoring + selection is a stronger MLLM recipe than direct timestamp generation (consistent with RQ3), (3) the label is a popularity vote, so it inherits popularity bias; no debiasing is attempted.
