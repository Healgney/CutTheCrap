# A10 PodReels (arXiv 2311.05867, DIS 2024) -- reading notes

Read: arXiv HTML v2: abstract, intro, related work, formative study (3.1 "what makes a good teaser", 3.2 workflow and pain points incl. existing AI tools, 3.3-3.4 design goals), system (sentence-based pipeline, candidate generation, refine view), LLM evaluation, user-study summary. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2311.05867v2 . Appendix prompts only skimmed.

- [F] Task: help podcasters create 15/30/45-s "teasers" (default 30 s) from hour-long video podcast episodes; the paper reports a manual workflow of ~1 hour per 30-s teaser from a 1-h episode.
- [F] Formative study: audience comments on 7 example teasers (E1-E7) and interviews with podcast creators (C1-C5; counts of participants partly extracted: "seven out of ten participants", "eight out of ten audiences") yielded ingredients: brevity (15-45 s), strong hook (informational, curiosity-arousing, funny, emotional styles; authentic, not clickbait), clear context, high production quality.
- [F] Self-containedness language: "Providing clear context is crucial when creating self-contained video podcast teasers"; teaser should be understandable by a brand-new listener; creators note speakers use referents such as "here" and "them" that are meaningless out of context; 8/10 audience members felt confused by a teaser made of out-of-context mid-episode pieces (E7); a balance is needed between giving enough context and withholding details to keep curiosity. Creators routinely add neighbouring sentences "to make the main topic more evident" and remove filler/rambling sentences.
- [F] Existing AI tools (Clips.AI, Opus) were tried by two creators: Clips.AI returned ~80 candidates for a 2-h episode (too many; vague reasons); Opus capped output at 15 clips with scores but quality was inconsistent and its automatic edits looked generic. Design goal: few options, with explanations, and user control.
- [F] System: transcription with word-level timing, active speaker diarization, filler-word detection, audio-event detection; sentence is the basic edit unit "because sentences have a natural starting and ending point of speech and often express a complete idea"; GPT-4 receives the transcript and user criteria (length, featured speakers, style, keywords) and returns sentence IDs which are mapped back to timestamps; tagline generated by the LLM; refine view lets users toggle sentences and expand context windows before/after.
- [F] Evaluation: (1) LLM adherence to criteria checked by human annotators (multi-parameter test: 30 clips; single-parameter test: 3 clips per option): single-parameter accuracy duration 86.1%, speakers 88.9%, keywords 87.5%; multi-parameter: 83.3% duration, 83.3% speakers, 76.7% of clips correct on >=3 criteria; curiosity-arousing style perfect. (2) Within-subjects study, 10 creators (5 expert / 5 novice) vs a text-edit baseline: teasers in 59% of the time with 44% of the mental demand.
- [F] Nothing in the paper measures engagement outcomes of the teasers (no views/retention labels); no benchmark or dataset is released; no audio ablation (audio events/diarization are inputs only).
- [V] No public code found; Adobe later shipped a "Clip Maker" feature (Adobe Research news page; marketing source, not used as evidence).
