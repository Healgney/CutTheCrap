# D13 TripleSumm: Adaptive Triple-Modality Fusion for Video Summarization (MoSu dataset)
Kim, Jeong, Kang, Kim, Oh, Lee (Seoul National Univ.). arXiv 2603.01169v1 (1 Mar 2026). Venue: ICLR 2026 [F: GitHub README "accepted to ICLR 2026"; arXiv page itself does not name a venue].
Reading depth: full text of the arXiv HTML read locally (main text Sec 1-5, Appendix A, B.1-B.6, E.1-E.2, G.2, H skimmed; appendix tail after B.4 and tables partly garbled in extraction). Not read: Tab. XII-style details beyond what is listed below.

## 1. Task definition [F]
Frame-level importance regression (score in [0,1] per second) from three synchronised streams (visual, text/transcript, audio), then standard summary generation: KTS shot segmentation, shot score = mean frame score, 0/1 knapsack at a 15% length budget (Sec 3.3, App A.2). Highlight detection is evaluated on 5-s segments by mAP50/mAP15.

## 2. Label source and construction [F]
- Labels = YouTube "Most Replayed" statistic, following Mr.HiSum; 100 scores per video over uniformly segmented clips; described as a normalised frequency of replay counts from "tens of thousands of views" (App B.2). No human annotation. How 100 bins are mapped to 1-s frames is not spelled out in the text I read [V].
- Source: YouTube-8M. Filters (Sec 4): English transcript available (original or via YouTube's automatic caption translation), audio track, >50,000 views, >=120 s. Videos without transcripts excluded (App B.1).
- Scale: 52,678 videos, ~3,983.7 h (Tab. 1); duration mean 272.25 s (sd 102.4), range 120-~501 s (Tab. II / Fig. I). Transcript density 61.84% of video duration, mean 619.1 tokens/video, 32.6M tokens total (Tab. II). 10 thematic clusters (App B.3, e.g. Video Games 11,578 videos); stratified 8:1:1 split per cluster.
- Category count is stated inconsistently: 3,862 knowledge-graph classes (Sec 4) vs 3,406 (Tab. 1). Views wording also differs ("over 50,000 views" vs "at least 50,000 viewers"). [F, paper-internal inconsistencies]

## 3. Bias sources and the authors' treatment [F]
- Position/onset: App B.2 "Handling YouTube Most Replayed Bias": an anomalous spike in replay score within the first few seconds, often before the main content begins, attributed to heightened initial attention or buffering adjustments; if untreated the model could tie importance to temporal position instead of content. Treatment: ground-truth scores of the first 5 s of every video are set to 0. No figure or number quantifying the spike is given in what I read [F by absence]; no sensitivity analysis of the 5-s choice.
- Popularity: the 50k-view threshold is justified as statistical reliability (following Mr.HiSum; cites Kim et al. 2025b) not as bias correction.
- Duration: restricted to 120-~501 s; no duration-bias analysis. Channel, title, thumbnail, genre-level replay behaviour: not addressed. Per-cluster performance is reported (App G.2, Fig. IV, no numbers; Sports and Animation&Comics higher medians).
- The paper does not discuss self-fulfilling replay bias, partial-replay ambiguity or the music/onset artifact.

## 4. Assumptions of the debiasing step [I]
The zeroing assumes the artifact is confined to <=5 s at the start and that real highlights do not occur there; it is a deterministic mask, not a propensity model. It cannot correct a position effect that decays smoothly over the first tens of seconds, or seek-back effects after chapter markers. No identifiability/propensity assumptions (no IPS).

## 6. Modality ablations [F]
Tab. 4a (MoSu, tau/rho/mAP50/mAP15): visual only 0.298/0.404/72.20/40.24; text only 0.267/0.364/70.01/37.37; audio only 0.271/0.370/69.96/37.88; three two-modality rows 0.307/0.416/72.41/41.20, 0.328/0.442/73.77/42.39, 0.331/0.448/73.85/42.64 (checkmark columns were not recoverable from the extraction, so which pair is which is [V]); all three 0.351/0.472/74.72/44.42. So adding both text and audio to visual gains +0.053 tau; text and audio are each worth ~0.27 tau alone (i.e. transcript and audio alone are only slightly below vision).
Other ablations: window schedule (Local-to-Global best), MST/CMF blocks (one block alone 0.250 / 0.337 vs both 0.351), fusion (static 0.338, global 0.344, dynamic 0.351). App E.1 Tab. XII ranks modalities per frame by attention and drops them: rank-1 only 0.196/0.273/66.09/33.28; rank1+2 0.273/0.373/70.28/38.69.
Features: visual CLIP ViT-L/14 at 1 fps; text = RoBERTa-base [CLS] per YouTube transcript sentence, spread across spanned seconds, default <pad> vector when no text, non-verbal tags like [Music] removed; audio = Audio Spectrogram Transformer (MIT/ast-finetuned-audioset-10-10-0.4593) on a 10-s window around each second (App B.1). External benchmarks use Qwen2-VL-7B/Qwen2.5-VL captions as text (App B.1 vs Sec 5.1 differ on version) [F]. No comment/danmaku/uploader inputs.
Important for RQ5 [I]: ablation is run on Most Replayed labels, so it shows what predicts replay, not what predicts clip quality; transcripts are YouTube captions (original or auto), ASR quality not evaluated.

## 8. Self-stated limitations [F]
No dedicated limitations section. Conclusion: follows the 3-step protocol (frame scoring, segmentation, selection) and suggests end-to-end as future work; Sec 5.4 notes transcripts are sparse in in-the-wild videos; long-video test set small (50).

## 9. Difference from our direction [I]
Same weak-label family (Most Replayed) and the closest explicit "bias" handling, but: (i) videos are 2-8 min, features at 1 s, outputs are frame scores, not 15-90 s self-contained clips from 30 min-hours speech-first video; (ii) debiasing = 5-s zero mask, without evidence, estimation, or test on human labels; (iii) no title/thumbnail/channel/duration treatment; (iv) the multimodal ablation measures replay prediction, not clip value; (v) long-video generalisation is evaluated against Most Replayed again, so any bias is shared by train and test.
