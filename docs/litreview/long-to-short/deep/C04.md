# C04 Rhapsody: A Dataset for Highlight Detection in Podcasts
Park, Diwan, Harwath, Choi. COLM 2025; arXiv 2505.19429 (v1 26 May 2025, v2 7 Sep 2025). Read: full HTML text (Sections 1-6, appendix A.2-A.3 headings, Tables 1-4), GitHub README. reading_depth = full-text (appendix figures not read).

## 1) Task definition
- [F] Podcast highlight detection: given an episode, predict which of 100 equal-duration segments are highlights. Inputs: episode title, per-segment transcript summaries (LLM-generated), optional audio features. Output: set of segment indices P compared to ground-truth set G. Treated as document retrieval (P = retrieved, G = relevant).
- [F] Motivation: highlights help listeners get the gist of an episode; replay-graph work so far was visual.

## 2) Labels / annotation source and agreement
- [F] Labels derive from YouTube "Most replayed" replay graph: 100 time-aligned scores in [0,1], available for videos with typically >=50K views. GT = at most K=20 indices with score > tau=0.5; K and tau chosen "based on manual data inspection". Typical |G| is about 5 (5.32 in Table 4).
- [F] Position-bias correction: replay graphs are biased towards the first segment (users replay beginnings); 75% of episodes had a GT highlight at segment 1; fixed by setting the first value equal to the second (average highlights 5.86 to 5.20).
- [F] No human annotation, no annotator agreement, no human validation of the replay-derived labels. Authors state the task is subjective with derived gold labels so the upper bound may be imperfect.
- [F] Collection: popular-podcast channels on YouTube; keep playlists set as podcasts by creators, English, not predominantly visual (no music videos, TV reviews, reaction videos), replay graph available, episodes up to 1 h. 13,364 episodes, 90 channels; avg 27.21 min, 4.71k words, 0.55M views; split 70:10:20; test = 2,467 episodes with at least one GT highlight.

## 3) Self-containedness / context
- [F] Not addressed: segments are equal-time chunks (a word goes to a segment only if its start and end fall inside, else to the larger overlap); sentence alignment and whether a segment is understandable alone are not discussed. Models see all 100 segment summaries at once (full-episode context), so a predicted segment is judged in context.

## 4) Unit / boundaries
- [F] 100 equal-duration segments (about 16 s at the mean length; the paper gives no seconds figure, so this is my derivation [I]). No boundary accuracy is evaluated.

## 5) Metrics and key numbers (Table 2, test set, 2,467 episodes)
- Metrics: hit rate (at least one predicted index in G), precision, recall, F1, AP (formulas not given). Prediction thresholds for BM25 and the fine-tuned models were tuned on validation to roughly match average prediction counts (0.3, 0.425, 0.375 for text-only, DVA, HuBERT); zero-shot LLMs cannot be controlled, so the authors only ensure they do not predict excessively many.
- Hit rate / precision / recall / F1 / AP: Random 0.220/0.053/0.052/0.039/0.021; Frequency 0.224/0.072/0.064/0.051/0.031; BM25 0.216/0.051/0.060/0.040/0.024; GPT-4o text 0.265/0.088/0.053/0.057/0.032; Gemini text 0.327/0.082/0.074/0.065/0.037; Gemini text+audio 0.293/0.090/0.065/0.063/0.040; FT Llama text 0.452/0.202/0.178/0.163/0.134; +DVA 0.477/0.216/0.187/0.175/0.140; +HuBERT 0.490/0.266/0.197/0.197/0.153.
- [F] Seeds / CI / significance: only random and frequency baselines are averaged (1,000 runs); no confidence intervals or tests for any other system; fine-tuned runs not described as repeated.

## 6) Modality ablation (audio vs transcript)
- [F] Audio features per segment: wav2vec2-based dominance-valence-arousal emotion model (1024-d final hidden layer) and HuBERT layer 10 average-pooled (512-d); audio is 16 kHz split into the same 100 segments.
- [F] Fine-tuned Llama-3.2-1B (QLoRA, sigmoid head per segment): hit rate text 0.452 -> +DVA 0.477 (+0.025) -> +HuBERT 0.490 (+0.038); F1 0.163 -> 0.175 -> 0.197; AP 0.134 -> 0.140 -> 0.153. These are single reported numbers without CIs [V].
- [F] Genre breakdown (Table 3, hit rate text / +DVA / +HuBERT): Entertainment 0.489/0.541/0.642; News & Politics 0.384/0.403/0.427; Comedy 0.714/0.714/0.820; Sci & Tech 0.313/0.374/0.420; Education 0.326/0.341/0.444; Sports 0.586/0.441/0.649; People & Blogs 0.527/0.516/0.637. HuBERT beats text-only in every genre; DVA hurts in Sports and People & Blogs and is flat in Comedy.
- [F] Zero-shot Gemini with audio is lower than text-only in hit rate (0.293 vs 0.327), though precision/AP are slightly higher; the paper says audio "does not consistently" help; the genre table shows text+audio below text-only in all seven genres for Gemini.
- [I] Gain from audio exists but is generic embedding (averaged HuBERT), no laughter/prosody analysis; confounded with fine-tuning (the audio only helps once a model is trained on in-domain labels).

## 8) Limitations stated
- No dedicated section. Pipeline components (ASR, summarization, audio features, prediction) may compound errors, suggesting joint training; labels are derived and subjective, upper bound uncertain; replay graph structure and genre-adaptive strategies listed as future work; English only; harmful content warning.

## 9) Differences from our direction
- Closest behaviour-labelled podcast highlight set (RQ2) and the only controlled podcast audio-gain number (RQ5: +3.8 hit-rate points with HuBERT).
- But: fixed 100-segment grid (about 16 s), no sentence/turn/shot-aligned boundaries, no self-containedness, no hook title, no K-ranked independent clips; hit rate (at least one of ~N predictions in G) is a lenient set-level metric; labels carry position bias that they only partly correct (first segment) and no channel-popularity or thumbnail debiasing; audio-only podcasts hosted as video on YouTube, so replay labels also reflect the video-platform audience.
