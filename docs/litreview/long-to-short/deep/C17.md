# C17 Extractive is not Faithful: An Investigation of Broad Unfaithfulness Problems in Extractive Summarization
Zhang, Wan, Bansal. ACL 2023 (pp. 2153-2174); arXiv 2209.03549. Read: full text incl. Appendices D-F and Limitations (own text dump of ar5iv); repo README. reading_depth = full-text (appendix tables A-C only skimmed).

## 1) Task definition
- [F] Analyse errors that arise when sentences or elementary discourse units are extracted and concatenated in original order into a summary of a news article. Five "broad unfaithfulness" types (Figure 2): incorrect coreference (anaphor refers to a different entity than in the document), incomplete coreference (anaphor with ambiguous or no antecedent), incorrect discourse (linking term or unit falsely connects to neighbouring context), incomplete discourse (linking term/unit lacks needed preceding/following context), other misleading information.
- [F] Anaphors counted: pronouns (they, she, he, it, this, that, those, these, them, her, him, their, his ...) and "determiner + noun" phrases. A determiner-NP with no antecedent that does not affect understanding is not counted (footnote 5).
- [F] Discourse: linking terms (but, and, also, meanwhile, however ...) per Penn Discourse Treebank; for EDU-level systems every unit potentially needs a neighbour (RST). Implicit discourse relations without a linking term are not annotated (too ambiguous).
- [F] Evaluation target: detect these problems automatically (ExtEval) and meta-evaluate against human labels.

## 2) Labels / annotation source and agreement
- [F] 100 CNN/DM test articles x 16 extractive systems (10 supervised incl. two oracles; 6 unsupervised incl. Lead3) = 1,600 summaries. CNN/DM is Apache 2.0.
- [F] Four annotators (two PhD-student authors, two CS undergraduate NLP researchers), about 3 months. Each summary labelled by two annotators independently with five yes/no questions plus pointing to the offending span; SpanBERT coreference clusters (AllenNLP 2.4.0) were colour-highlighted as an aid (annotators told to use with caution). Mechanical Turk was deliberately not used because coreference/discourse need linguistic background.
- [F] Aggregation: coreference and discourse disagreements were resolved jointly ("little subjectivity"); misleading was double-checked individually and counted only if both annotators said yes (79 of 149 flagged). Priority rule to avoid double counting: incorrect coref = incorrect discourse > incomplete coref = incomplete discourse > misleading.
- [F] Inter-annotator agreement: no kappa or raw agreement is reported (only the 149 vs 79 counts for misleading).

## 3) How "standalone / dangling" is defined and measured
- [F] Definition = Figure 2 (above); the dangling-anaphora notion is cited from Gupta and Lehal 2010 (introduction). The paper treats the extract as a whole read in order, not as independently readable sentences (footnote 4).
- [F] Human rates (Section 3.3): any problem 30.3% (484/1600); incomplete coref 15.4% (247); incomplete discourse 10.7% (171); misleading 4.9% (79); incorrect coref 3.9% (63); incorrect discourse 1.1% (18). Oracle systems worst (they pick middle-of-document sentences), Lead3 best.
- [F] Automatic detector ExtEval (Section 4.3, Appendix F), ExtEval = IncorCorefEval + IncomCorefEval + IncomDiscoEval + SentiBias:
  - IncomCorefEval: run SpanBERT coreference on the summary and on the full document; if the first mention of a summary cluster is a pronoun or determiner-NP and it is not the first mention of the corresponding document cluster -> flag. Closed word lists (pronouns and determiners listed above; "frequent terms in our dataset, not exhaustive").
  - IncomDiscoEval: closed list of linking terms (and, so, still, also, however, but, clearly, meanwhile, not only, not just, on one side, on another, then, moreover); flag if the term is present but the needed previous/next sentence is absent, or an EDU lacks its previous unit in the same sentence; cannot separate incorrect from incomplete discourse.
  - IncorCorefEval: same mention mapped to different clusters in document vs summary.
- [F] Human correlation (Table 1, n=1,600, Pearson r / Spearman rho): IncomCorefEval vs incomplete-coref label 0.48/0.48; IncomDiscoEval vs incomplete-discourse label 0.61/0.61; IncorCorefEval vs incorrect-coref 0.25/0.25; ExtEval vs Overall 0.54/0.46; best existing metric BERTScore-Precision vs Overall 0.37/0.35; ROUGE-2-F1 -0.04/0.02, FactCC 0.11/0.05, DAE 0.05/0.07, QuestEval 0.17/0.19. Note IncomDiscoEval vs Overall 0.42/0.38 and IncomCorefEval vs Overall 0.42/0.42.
- [F] Repair check (Section 5): of 32 detected incorrect coreferences 28 were true (87.5% precision); 60 were human-labelled so recall 46.7%; a replace-with-first-mention program fixed 16 of 28.
- [F] SummEval subset (4 extractive systems, expert consistency scores): ExtEval had the best Spearman (Appendix B; numbers not extracted).

## 8) Limitations stated
- Findings most relevant where extractive methods work well (CNN/DM) and not extreme abstractive tasks (XSum); expert annotators needed, crowd scale-up would need training; ExtEval not applicable to abstractive summaries except SentiBias; percentages may change with another dataset (23 PubMed oracle summaries manually checked: 2 incorrect coref, 5 incomplete coref, 1 incorrect discourse, 1 incomplete discourse).

## 9) Differences from our direction, and can the detector run on ASR transcripts? (assignment (b), evidence only from this paper)
- Evidence available: all numbers (r=0.48 for incomplete coreference, 0.61 for incomplete discourse, precision 87.5%/recall 46.7% for incorrect coreference) come from punctuated, capitalised, edited English news with the full source document available. The paper contains no speech, ASR, dialogue or noisy-text experiment (the only other domain check is 23 PubMed summaries inspected by hand, no metric).
- Mechanism dependencies: (i) a coreference model run on both the extract and the full document (our clip vs the full transcript is analogous, since both are available), (ii) hand-made closed lists of pronouns and linking terms derived from CNN/DM, "not exhaustive" -- spoken language uses discourse markers (so, yeah, like, I mean, and then) not in the list [I], (iii) sentence boundaries for the "previous/next sentence" check, which ASR transcripts lack unless punctuation is restored [I].
- Verdict [I]: the logic transfers, the validation does not. A usable first step is to re-implement IncomCoref / IncomDisco on clip-vs-episode transcript pairs and validate against our own human labels; the published correlations cannot be quoted as expected performance on ASR.
- Other differences: summaries for reading vs clips for viewing; no engagement; no boundaries chosen (units are given); no audio.
