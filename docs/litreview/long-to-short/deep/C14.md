# C14 Decontextualization: Making Sentences Stand-Alone
Choi, Palomaki, Lamm, Kwiatkowski, Das, Collins. TACL 2021; arXiv 2102.05169. Read: full text (ar5iv, own text dump) Sections 1-8, Tables 1-4; dataset README (GitHub). reading_depth = full-text.

## 1) Task definition
- [F] Given a sentence s in a Wikipedia page, decide whether it can be decontextualized (feasible) and if so produce s' that is interpretable in the empty context and has the same truth-conditional meaning in the empty context as s has in its context (Definition 1; Section 2). Implicatures are excluded (closer to explicature).
- [F] Operational edits: annotators may only copy a few phrases from the page into the sentence and delete phrases ("minor modifications"); addition of background is optional; hard cases (often narrative or sentences depending heavily on the preceding few sentences) go to "infeasible" (Section 3).

## 2) Labels / annotation source and agreement
- [F] Data: English Wikipedia. Sampling: a Natural Questions (question, page, short answer) triple uniformly at random; the sentence containing the short answer is one example and a random sentence from the page is a second; excluded plot sections, first sentences, single-sentence paragraphs (Section 3/4).
- [F] Sizes: train 11,290 (1 annotation each), dev 1,945 and test 1,945 (5 annotations each), expert set 100 (4-way). 28 US-based native-English crowd annotators; ~4 min per example.
- [F] Agreement on the category decision: Fleiss kappa 0.51 among experts and 0.30 among crowd; binary (feasible/infeasible) agreement 85%. The authors note some crowdworkers had a loose notion of "stand alone" and attempted decontextualization everywhere. Edit-level: about 32.5% of one annotator edits are covered by another annotator edits (many valid rewrites).
- [F] Quality check: of 100 crowd annotations, 89% valid for content and 88% for form.

## 3) Definition and measurement of "stand-alone"
- [F] Definition: Section 2 (above). Measurement: human judgment of whether the sentence is "fluent, unambiguous and clear when posed alone" (annotator instruction); no explicit test is given for when an edit is needed; Table 1 lists edit types instead.
- [F] Distribution (text of Section 4): infeasible roughly 10% of data; over 60% of sentences both require decontextualization and can be decontextualized; only about 30% are interpretable out of context without any edit. (Table 3 in my text dump is garbled, so per-split shares are not re-derived [V].)
- [F] Edit types from 200 manually analysed examples (Table 1): pronoun/NP swap 40.5%, name completion 11.5%, discourse-marker removal 3.5%, bridging modifier 13%, global scoping 7%, optional background 10%.
- [F] Automatic: T5 with a category prefix (unnecessary / feasible / infeasible); feasible-vs-infeasible accuracy: always-feasible 88%, T5-11B 89% (predicts infeasible for 5% vs humans 12%), humans 85%, T5-Base 77%. A SpanBERT coreference baseline modifies 36.5% of examples. No correlation between an automatic score and human stand-alone judgments is reported beyond these accuracies.

## 4) Unit / boundaries
- [F] Unit = one Wikipedia sentence given its page; models see title, section titles and the paragraph only; annotators said 10-20% of examples needed the full document (Section 3.4/5).
- [F] Multi-sentence outputs are left as future work.

## 5) Metrics and key numbers
- [F] Metrics: length increase, % edited, sentence match (normalized match against any reference; on all and on edited-only examples), SARI add/delete P/R/F1 with fractional counts over references.
- [F] Dev (Table 4): Repeat 38/0 match, 0 SARI; Coref 39/13, add-F1 22, del-F1 31; T5-Base 48/21, 29, 40; T5-11B 53/32, 42, 46; Human 45/29, 56, 58. Test T5-11B 52/32, SARI 43/47; human 44/28, 56/58.
- [F] Human eval, 2 expert raters, 100 examples (Section 5.2.2): "fluent and grammatically correct?" T5 88.0% vs human 84.5%; "sufficiently and correctly decontextualized?" T5 67.5% vs human 78.5%; both raters preferred human outputs; agreed on 37 of 40 cases with a preference.
- [F] Applications (Section 6): 150 questions x 10 ratings; decontextualized sentences preferred over original sentences (log-odds 0.85, CI [0.4, 1.3]) and over paragraphs (0.55, CI [0.1, 1.0]); original vs paragraph n.s. Retrieval: decontextualized sentences approach 100-word-window recall at ~1/10 the cost (7k docs, 233k paragraphs, 868k sentences).

## 8) Limitations stated
- Encyclopedic text only (newswire, literary, social media, technical text harder); single sentences only; English Wikipedia and US annotators; models see only title+paragraph; T5 relies on title cues, misses ambiguous non-main entities; SARI absolute scores hard to interpret.

## 9) Differences from our direction
- Sentence-level, written Wikipedia, rewriting allowed; we need a score for an unmodifiable 15-90 s spoken clip chosen from a long recording, plus a decision where to start/end.
- Gives a formal definition (interpretable in the empty context; same meaning) that can be re-used for the first sentence(s) of a clip and a three-way label (stand-alone / needs fix / infeasible) with a documented base rate (~30% stand-alone in Wikipedia).
- Agreement is modest even for sentence-level written text (kappa 0.30 crowd / 0.51 expert) -> expect a noisy label for spoken clips.
- No engagement, no boundaries, no speech/ASR phenomena (disfluency, missing punctuation).
