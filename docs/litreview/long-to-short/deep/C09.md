# C09 Comparing Lexical, Acoustic/Prosodic, Structural and Discourse Features for Speech Summarization
Maskey, Hirschberg. Interspeech 2005 (Eurospeech), pp. 621-624. Read: full 4-page paper (publisher-independent Columbia copy, converted with pdftotext in scratchpad, not in the project). reading_depth = full-text. Old, news-domain; included as the classic quantified prosody-ablation.

## 1) Task definition
- [F] Extractive summarization of Broadcast News stories as binary classification of each sentence (in summary or not); compare feature classes.
## 2) Labels / annotation source and agreement
- [F] 216 stories from 20 CNN shows of TDT-2 (about 10 hours audio); manual transcripts, Dragon ASR transcripts and audio; extractive summaries per story annotated by a single annotator (the same annotator for all stories); no agreement reported. Sentence, turn and topic segmentation from TDT-2.
## 3) Self-containedness
- [F] Not addressed. Discourse "givenness" feature (new vs given noun stems) is the only discourse-related notion: given information is less likely to be in a summary (redundancy, not intelligibility).
## 4) Unit
- [F] Sentence (as segmented in TDT-2); sentence duration in seconds is itself a feature.
## 5) Metrics and key numbers
- [F] Bayesian Network classifier, 10-fold cross-validation with resampling, feature selection; baseline = first 23% of sentences (mean summary length): F 0.43, P 0.43, R 0.43.
- [F] F-measure by feature set alone: discourse 0.13, structural 0.33, acoustic/prosodic 0.47, lexical 0.49. Lexical+acoustic 0.52; +structural 0.54; +discourse 0.544; all four: F 0.54, recall 0.61, precision 0.49, accuracy 73.8% (11% above baseline). Acoustic+structural only: F 0.50 (the paper's "no transcription needed" claim; note sentence and speaker segmentation are still assumed). ROC AUC by classifier: BayesNet 0.771, C4.5 0.647, Ripper 0.643, SVM 0.535. Correlation of lexical-based and acoustic-based predictions 0.74.
- [F] ROUGE (Fig. 2): best ROUGE-1/ROUGE-L 0.85 (27% over baseline), mean over ROUGE variants 0.80 (30.3% above baseline); acoustic+structural alone ROUGE-1 0.76, mean 0.68.
## 6) Modality ablation
- [F] Acoustic/prosodic alone F 0.47 vs lexical 0.49; lexical+acoustic 0.52 vs lexical alone 0.49 (+0.03 F). Acoustic features: speaking rate, F0 min/max/mean/range/slope, RMS energy min/max/mean/slope, sentence duration, speaker-normalised (normalised better than raw). Feature selection: sentence duration (timeLen), minDB, maxDB most discriminative; pitch features "curiously" among the least useful; the single best feature overall is time length in seconds. So most of the acoustic signal is duration and energy, not pitch prosody [I from Table 1 and text].
## 7) Data / code / license
- [F] TDT-2 corpus (LDC); no code or license statement in the paper.
## 8) Limitations stated
- Strict exact-match evaluation (they add ROUGE); named entities assumed from an external tagger; speaker-type feature binary and conflates anchors with interviewees.
## 9) Differences from our direction
- News anchors/reporters (professional speakers), 216 stories, sentence classification, single annotator; no engagement; no boundaries chosen; no clip-level standalone concept. Value: quantified evidence that duration/energy/prosody features alone are about as predictive as lexical features for sentence salience in broadcast speech (0.47 vs 0.49 F), but gain over lexical+structural is small (+0.03 F for adding acoustic to lexical). Not evidence for podcasts.
