## 1) Task definition
- [F] Two tasks from one label: (a) video highlight detection -- score frames, split the video into 5-s shots (mean frame score), top 15% or 50% shots = GT highlight, metric MAP; (b) video summarisation -- KTS shot boundaries, 0/1 knapsack under a 15% length budget, metric F1 (Zhang et al. protocol).
- [F] Input: YouTube-8M frame features at 1 fps (Inception-v3, PCA to 1024-d), videos 121-300 s (mean 201.9 s). Output: per-frame importance. No audio, text or comments; no K clips, no duration target other than the percentage budgets.

## 2) Label source and construction
- [F] Label = YouTube "Most replayed" distribution: a public feature showing how often each part was re-watched, aggregated across all viewers; supplied as 100 uniformly spaced normalised scores in [0,1] per video (e.g. 6-s bins for a 600 s video), linearly aligned to 1-fps features. Selection: YouTube-8M videos with >=50,000 views (so the statistic is not driven by a few users), non-deleted, <=300 s, music-category excluded (replay depends on audio there; task is visual) -> 31,892 videos, 1,788 h, 3,509 entity categories (153 with >=100 videos), views 50k-290M.
- [F] Reliability evidence: (1) cross-dataset transfer -- training on 22,743 realistic Mr.HiSum videos: SL-module MAP on TVSum 71.3 (target-only) vs 69.5 zero-shot vs 73.0 fine-tuned; YouTube Highlights 59.9 vs 57.9 vs 62.0; PGL-SUM/VASNet F1 on TVSum 55.5/54.2 -> 57.1/57.1, on SumMe 41.7/41.9 -> 42.3/42.6; (2) user study: 25 videos x 10 clips of 5-10 s (5 highlight/5 non-highlight by the label), 30 annotators, 6 votes per clip -> 64.8% agree (23.6% 6:0, 24.4% 5:1, 16.8% 4:2), 15.6% 3:3, 19.6% disagree.
- [F] Biases admitted: self-fulfilling prophecy (Most Replayed UI shows after >=50k views, so later viewers may replay what others replayed; stats for the first 50k viewers are not available separately); unknown counting of partial replays (YouTube does not disclose); authors argue limited impact because only explicit replays count.
- [I] Unadmitted: 100-bin resolution (3 s bins at 300 s; ~36 s bins at 1 h) so unusable for second-level boundaries on long videos; popularity filter (>=50k views) and YT8M sampling; replay peaks reflect "interesting" and also "confusing/need to rewatch", intro replays and seek-bar behaviour; visual-only so replay spikes caused by speech/humor/audio are unexplained (authors drop music for this reason).

## 3) How start/end are determined
- [F] No start/end prediction. Highlights are the top 15%/50% of fixed 5-s shots; summary segments are KTS (kernel temporal segmentation) shots chosen by knapsack. Boundary quality is not evaluated (Mr.HiSum ignores boundaries by design).

## 4) Self-containedness / context completeness
- 无 (none). The paper explicitly says highlight scores differ from summary-importance scores (a visually beautiful scene may be a highlight but not part of a story summary), which is a different issue from context-independence.

## 5) Evaluation and key numbers
- [F] Table 6 (2,000-video test, 5 seeds): PGL-SUM F1 55.89, MAP50 61.60, MAP15 27.45; VASNet 55.26/58.69/25.28; SL-module 55.31/58.63/24.95; DSNet 50.78/57.31/24.35; iPTNet 50.53/55.53/22.74.
- [F] Variance on small sets: TVSum 5-fold x 5 seeds PGL-SUM F1 ranges 49.0-60.7 (Table 2); validation-set-size study (25-2,000 videos) shows unstable curves for small sets; authors conclude >=1,000 validation videos are needed for robust evaluation. Category study (Food/Vehicle/Game, training size 25-2,000): Food saturates with far fewer videos than Vehicle or Video Game.

## 8) Self-stated limitations
- [F] Replay counting for partial rewatches is undefined; self-fulfilling bias after the UI appears; summary vs highlight labels are not identical (they argue a correlation).

## 9) Difference from our direction
- Provides the reference case of behaviour-derived dense labels with validated agreement, and the clearest author-side discussion of a behaviour-label bias (self-fulfilling replays). Differences: 2-5 min videos, visual-only features, 100-bin heatmap, no clip proposals or K, no boundaries, no self-containedness, no speech; high Most-Replayed on a long podcast would be too coarse unless YouTube's heatmap resolution for long videos (also 100 bins [I, from this paper's 100-bin description only for videos <=300 s]) is analysed -- unresolved [V]. Useful as an auxiliary pretraining/validation signal for "moments people replay" but not as clip-level supervision for long speech content.
