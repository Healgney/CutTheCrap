# C19 BooookScore: A systematic exploration of book-length summarization in the era of LLMs
Chang, Lo, Goyal, Iyyer. ICLR 2024; arXiv 2310.00785 (v4, 13 Apr 2024). Read: full HTML text (own dump incl. the previously unread tail: Section 6 Limitations, Appendices H-J). reading_depth = full-text (long prompt appendices skimmed).

## 1) Task definition
- [F] Evaluate coherence of LLM summaries of book-length documents (>100K tokens) produced by hierarchical merging vs incremental updating of chunk summaries. Contribution relevant to us: a coherence-error taxonomy from human annotation and an automatic LLM-based metric (BooookScore).

## 2) Labels / annotation source and agreement
- [F] Human annotation (Section 3): 4 annotators with proofreading experience hired on Upwork, each annotating 25 disjoint GPT-4 summaries (100 summaries of 100 recently published books, chosen to avoid contamination); they highlight confusing spans and write a question for each; about 30 min and $15 per summary; 1,193 span annotations (840 incremental, 353 hierarchical).
- [F] No chance-corrected agreement and no overlapping annotations: the authors argue recall cannot be measured and fine-grained annotators show low recall (Goyal et al. 2022; Dou et al. 2022); instead they measure precision of annotations by a second human pass (Section 4.1, Appendix J): the same four annotators validate annotations on the 25 books they did not annotate, for human and GPT-4 annotations mixed and origin hidden; 1,659 annotations validated, 79.7% of human spans legitimate. Criteria: (1) span is confusing; (2) question is relevant to the span, not answered anywhere in the summary explicitly or implicitly, and addresses an issue that would make the summary incoherent or hard to follow. Precision counts agree plus partially agree (partial = span confusing but questions fail some standard). Cost $418.85-418.90 ($1.7 per summary + $0.15 per annotation).

## 3) How "missing context / coherence" is defined and measured
- [F] Taxonomy (Table 1; eight types): entity omission (entity mentioned but key context/details missing), event omission, causal omission (reason/motivation missing), discontinuity, salience, language, inconsistency, duplication. Taxonomy derived from GPT-4 errors only.
- [F] BooookScore = fraction of summary sentences with no detected error; GPT-4 is prompted sentence by sentence with the preceding summary context and two few-shot summaries (42 sentence-level annotations); definitions and examples were critical for precision; whole-summary prompting produced trivial questions and low alignment with humans (Section 6).
- [F] Validation: GPT-4 annotations precision 78.2% vs human annotations 79.7% (both by human validation with the lenient agree+partial rule); system-level BooookScore with human vs LLM annotations 82.1 vs 82.4 (incremental) and 89.4 vs 90.8 (hierarchical) after treating consecutive-sentence spans and relations as single sentences. Existing reference-free metrics BLANC and SUPERT tested in Appendix I (not used further).
- [F] Caveat (Appendix H/Fig. 4): GPT-4 error-type distribution differs strongly from humans; it over-labels omission errors and often mislabels types; type predictions are "inaccurate" and not analysed.

## 4) Unit / boundaries
- [F] Unit = summary sentence (and spans of consecutive sentences for humans).

## 5) Metrics and key numbers
- [F] Section 5/Table 2: with 2048-token chunks hierarchical merging usually beats incremental updating (Claude 2: 91.1 vs 78.6); incremental improves with larger chunks (Claude 2 at 88K chunk 90.9 vs hierarchical 90.3); LLaMA-2-7B-Instruct far worse hierarchical BooookScore with heavy repeated trigrams.
- Cost: human annotation ~$3K plus $418.90 validation; LLM API ~$10K overall; authors claim BooookScore saves ~$15K and 500 hours.

## 8) Limitations stated (Section 6)
- Taxonomy derived only from GPT-4 errors; BooookScore uses GPT-4 as base LLM (possible systematic bias; pool of LLM judges suggested); expensive and slow (sentence-by-sentence, long prompt); no severity weights across error types; no recall validation and no overlapping annotations.

## 9) Differences from our direction
- LLM-judge template for "entity/event/causal omission" errors validated by span-level precision with human check; this is the most reusable evaluation recipe for a clip self-containedness score (judge reads clip transcript with preceding-context-free setting and flags confusing spans).
- But: abstractive book summaries, not excerpts of the source; validated precision only (lenient agree+partial), no recall and no inter-annotator kappa; the judge cannot name error types reliably; no speech, no engagement, no boundaries.
