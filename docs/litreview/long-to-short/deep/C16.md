# C16 A Question Answering Framework for Decontextualizing User-facing Snippets from Scientific Documents (QaDecontext)
Newman, Soldaini, Fok, Cohan, Lo. EMNLP 2023 (arXiv 2305.14772 v3). Read: full ar5iv text (own dump), Sections 1-6, Limitations, Ethics, Appendix A.2. reading_depth = full-text (appendix tables skimmed).

## 1) Task definition
- [F] Rewrite a snippet that will be shown outside its source (note-taking, search, citation graph explorers) so it is understandable alone while preserving meaning; user-facing requirements: multi-sentence snippets, preserve sentence count and roles, mark edits (brackets). Three-step framework: question generation, question answering over the source document, rewriting; QaDecontext = prompting strategy implementing it.

## 2) Labels / annotation source and agreement
- [F] Data: 289 snippets (avg 44.2 tokens) from S2ORC citation contexts (Citation Graph Explorer) and Qasper-based supporting passages (AI Research Assistant); 487 questions (avg 7.8 tokens), 487 answers (avg 20.7 tokens). Questions by four Upwork domain experts ($20/h); answers and rewrites by Prolific workers (US/UK, $17/h).
- [F] Question-type breakdown (Appendix A.2, annotator-labelled): define terms 31% in both sets; resolve coreference 37% (AI Research Assistant) and 44% (Citation Graph Explorer); seek more context 32% / 25%.
- [F] Agreement: for the human evaluation, two coauthors annotated 30 shared snippets and reached 70% binary agreement (they compare with 80% reported by Choi et al. for Wikipedia snippets). No agreement reported for data collection.

## 3) How out-of-context understandability is defined and measured
- [F] Definition: snippet understandable on its own with the reader outside the document; evaluated as "whether the snippet clarified the points the reader needed help understanding" (Section 5.3). Estimating need-for-context: in the data collection only about 20% of scientific snippets can be decontextualized from the snippet paragraph alone and about 50% when the abstract is added, versus 80-90% for Wikipedia (Table 5, Section 4/2.2).
- [F] Automatic: SARI-add against gold rewrites (authors note SARI favours fewer modifications and may not track human judgement). Human: two coauthors rank outputs best-to-worst (n=60) blind to system; 42% of gold decontextualizations judged acceptable; 45% of QaDecontext outputs still missing information or raising new questions (34% for gold); 24% bracket-format errors (5% for gold).

## 4) Unit / boundaries
- [F] Unit = snippet (a sentence or short passage, avg 44 tokens); boundaries are given.

## 5) Metrics and key numbers
- [F] SARI-add (Table 1): end-to-end gpt-4 0.103, davinci 0.135, QaDecontext 0.146 (41.7% relative gain over the gpt-4 end-to-end); oracle with gold questions/answers up to 0.527 (Table 2); question answering is the bottleneck; on Wikipedia QaDecontext is about 1 point below end-to-end prompting.
- [F] Human eval n=60: QaDecontext convincing 38% vs end-to-end 33% (binomial p=0.57, n.s.). Power analysis in Limitations: n=1,211 judgements per condition would be needed for a 5-point effect.

## 8) Limitations stated
- Automatic metrics (SARI) may not track human judgement; data collection expensive (experienced scientists took 5x longer on scientific than Wikipedia snippets), limiting baselines and human evaluation; closed-source commercial LLMs far better than open (tulu-30b, llama2-chat-70b); prompting does not reliably follow the guidelines; English NLP papers only; reformulation may introduce factual errors.

## 9) Differences from our direction
- Rewriting, not selecting; text; domain-specific scientific snippets; no boundaries/speech/engagement.
- Useful facts for RQ1: (i) need-for-context depends heavily on domain (20-50% of scientific snippets vs 80-90% Wikipedia are fixable from local context), (ii) users ask about coreference in 37-44% of their questions and about term definitions ~31%, i.e., both dangling references and missing definitions matter, (iii) even gold rewrites are judged acceptable only 42% of the time and human-human agreement is 70% on a small sample -> self-containedness labels are noisy.
