# A10 PodReels (arXiv 2311.05867v2; ACM DIS 2024) -- deep read

Read in full via arXiv HTML v2: abstract, intro, related work, formative study (Sec 3), system (Sec 4), technical evaluation (Sec 5), user study (Sec 6), discussion/limitations (Sec 7), prompt appendix. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2311.05867v2

## 1) Task definition
- [F] Human-AI co-creation of "video podcast teasers": short social clips that advertise a long (typically hour-long) video podcast episode. Input: full episode video, word-timed transcript, speaker diarization, filler words, audio events, face/body detection; plus user-chosen criteria (target length 15/30/45 s, featured speakers host/guest/both, style informational/curiosity/funny/emotional, keywords).
- [F] Output: 3 candidate "moments" per query, each a set of consecutive sentence IDs (single-moment teasers only), an LLM tagline (<10 words), then a user-refined sentence list that is rendered in Premiere Pro with transitions, music, captions. Default 30 s; teasers are not required to be contiguous after refinement (users reorder/remove sentences).
- [F] K is not fixed by the system: "three distinct and non-overlapping options" per query.

## 2) Label source and construction
- [F] There are no learned labels. Evidence base = a formative study: 7 teaser examples from popular YouTube podcast channels, 10 listeners (average age 23.4) gave likes/dislikes and whether the teaser made them want to watch the full episode; 5 expert teaser creators (avg ~2 years, >=50 teasers each) interviewed about principles and workflow.
- [F] The technical evaluation uses 4 YouTube trending podcast episodes (different genres, mean ~45 min); human annotators judge LLM outputs: duration checked by two authors against a 5-s margin (perfect agreement), style/keyword relevance rated 1-7 by 2 annotators per set (Cohen's kappa .61 and .63; >=5 counts as a match).
- [I] Known bias/limits: tiny sample (4 episodes, English only, speech-heavy low-visual-change videos), no engagement outcome measured, annotators are podcast enthusiasts, not the target audience.

## 3) How start/end points are determined
- [F] The sentence is the atomic unit ("natural starting and ending point of speech ... a complete idea/sub-idea"). GPT-4 returns sentence IDs only; the system maps them back to timestamps. Boundary precision metric: none except duration within +-5 s of the target length.
- [F] Refinement is human: the tool shows neighbouring sentences, finds the host question that leads to a guest answer (and vice versa), offers "Show Between" for far-apart context, a search box, drag-drop reordering, and a filler-word remover. Jump cuts created by removing sentences are detected and hidden with zoom or reaction-shot transitions.
- [F] Selection is among sentence-level candidate cut points (candidate-set formulation), not regression.

## 4) Self-containedness / context completeness
- [F] Treated explicitly in the formative study (Sec 3.1.3 "Clear context"): a good teaser must be understandable by a brand-new viewer; speakers' referents such as "here" or "them" are meaningless out of context; E7, built from out-of-context mid-episode pieces, confused 8 of 10 listeners; a teaser that gave no clarity on topic or whether the guest answered the question lost about half the audience's interest. Creators' refinement habit: pull in a few surrounding sentences "to make the main topic more evident" and delete rambling.
- [F] BUT the GPT-4 extraction prompt (appendix A.1.2) contains no context-independence instruction: it only asks for consecutive sentences around a target length, speaker set, style and keywords. Context is added only by the human in the refine step. [I] So self-containedness is a requirement identified by users and delegated to interaction, not modelled or measured.
- [F] Related design: hook styles (informational, curiosity-arousing, funny, emotional) are an input parameter; authenticity vs clickbait is discussed; the tagline is an LLM-generated short title (a rough analogue of our hook line), never evaluated.
- Metric: none for self-containedness.

## 5) Evaluation and key numbers
- [F] Table 3 (GPT-4 adherence): single-parameter accuracy duration 86.1%, speakers 88.9%, keywords 87.5%, style 77.1% (informational and emotional 83.3%, curiosity 100%, funny 41.7% with 7 of 12 failing); 168 clips total (36/36/48/48). Multi-parameter (30 random combinations out of 576): duration 83.3%, speakers 83.3%, style 73.3%, keywords 80.0%; 43.3% of clips score high on all four, 33.3% on three, 23.3% on two; 76.7% of clips succeed on >=3.
- [F] Within-subjects user study (n=10, 5 experts/5 novices) vs a baseline with sentence-level transcript editing but no extract/review/refine support: questionnaires on satisfaction, NASA-TLX, creativity support, usability, usefulness. Abstract/intro say teasers in 59% of the time with 44% of the mental demand; the Discussion states mental demand decreased by 41% and time by 56% -- the two statements are not consistent with each other [F: inconsistency in the text; I did not find the table values].
- No engagement-based (views/retention) metric, no comparison with human-chosen ground truth clips.

## 6) Modality ablation
- None ("无消融"). Audio events, diarization, face/body detection are pipeline inputs; diarization is used to filter by speaker and to find leading questions; no experiment removes any modality.

## 7) Data / code / licence
- [F] Paper is on arXiv under arXiv.org perpetual non-exclusive license. No dataset or code link in the paper text I read [V: not found in repo search]. Built as a Premiere Pro extension using GPT-4 API. Adobe later productised a "Clip Maker" (Adobe Research news page; marketing, not evidence).

## 8) Self-stated limitations
- [F] Only speech-heavy long-form videos with little visual change; English-only because filler-word and diarization components are English-only; single-moment teasers only (multi-moment teasers exist in the wild); small user study; Premiere Pro API limits access to raw frames; LLM may hallucinate (related-work remark); infeasible parameter combos (e.g., "funny" when nothing funny exists) are not detected -- future work (Sec 7.2).

## 9) Difference from our direction (long speech video -> K independent, watchable clips with aligned boundaries and a hook title)
- Same domain, similar clip length (15-45 s) and a hook-title analogue (tagline) -> high conceptual overlap; they also articulate that context-independence and hooks are the key quality aspects.
- Differences: (i) interactive creativity-support tool with GPT-4 prompting, no benchmark, no trained model, no released data; (ii) criteria-conditioned retrieval (length/speaker/style/keyword), not engagement- or behaviour-grounded ranking of K clips; (iii) self-containedness is delegated to humans (context-window expansion), not predicted or scored; (iv) no boundary-accuracy metric against reference clips; (v) outputs may be re-ordered/stitched sentences, not necessarily one contiguous clip; (vi) evaluation by criterion adherence + user study, not by agreement with published clips or viewer outcomes. Our contribution would need to be positioned as the measurable, benchmarked and learned version of what PodReels identifies qualitatively.
