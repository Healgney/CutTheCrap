## 1) Task definition
- [F] Query-agnostic video highlight detection in movies and TV dramas: score each 2-s clip in [0,1]; evaluated per clip. Inputs: visual (CLIP ViT-L-14 + SlowFast R-50) and audio (ImageBind). Output: clip-level highlight scores, no K, no duration constraint.
- [F, supplement Table A] Videos are SHORT: raw short-form videos from the platform avg 248.8 s (4,767 videos, ~65 shots); refined train set 1,368 videos avg 50.6 s (~30 shots, 3 scenes); test set 353 videos avg 134.9 s (~32 shots, 2 scenes). Total 51,890 shots, 4,673 scenes (as printed). So this is not a long-video benchmark.

## 2) Label source and construction
- [F] Training: collect ~5,000 high-engagement short videos derived from movies/TV on a short-video platform; LLM labels clips from titles and filters irrelevant ones; 2-s clip split; "community voting" = how often a clip is re-used across user-generated short videos, determined by video fingerprinting that traces popular clips to source videos; clip frequency aggregated into a vote score; the final training set (1,368 videos) keeps high-scoring crowd-validated clips and is used with VIDEO-LEVEL labels only (y=1 if at least one highlight). Clip-level pseudo-labels then come from MiniCPM-V 2.6 descriptions + DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen chain-of-thought (noisy), refined over 2 rounds.
- [F] Test: 353 held-out videos; multiple human annotators label 2-s highlights, majority vote; 171 highlight videos (59 action/combat, 16 impact/destruction, 36 CGI, 35 emotional climax, 25 others) and 182 non-highlight videos.
- [F] Release: the supplement says a curated subset (partial training sets with noisy labels + manually annotated test set) will be released after an anonymisation/copyright process; not yet available at the time of reading [V: no URL].
- [I] Biases: reuse frequency measures what editors of short videos like to re-cut (popularity + creator behaviour), skewed to salient action/CGI; platform and fingerprint-coverage selection; test labels are human but number/agreement of annotators is not given in the main text [V]. No debiasing.

## 3) How start/end are determined
- [F] Clip-level scoring only; the 2-s grid defines boundaries; no start/end regression and no boundary metric. MLLM baselines are tested in two modes (supplement B): direct moment retrieval ("identify all highlight segments ... start and end times") and per-clip scoring (rate each pre-cut clip 1-10); per-clip scoring wins by a large margin, which the authors attribute to MLLMs' poor temporal localisation.

## 4) Self-containedness / context completeness
- None ("无"). Local attention windows model neighbouring clips ("highlight degree depends on context") but there is no notion of understandable-without-context.

## 9) Difference from our direction
- Contributes one more instance of "downstream reuse of clips in short-video platforms = weak label for highlight value", with fingerprinting to map reuse back to sources (a plausible mechanism for deriving labels from published shorts of a podcast), plus evidence that per-clip scoring + selection beats MLLM timestamp generation (RQ3).
- Differences: (i) videos are short (50-135 s average), not hour-long, and are scripted visual media (fight/CGI/romance), not speech; (ii) task is dense clip scoring with AUC/AP, no start/end/ranking of K clips and no boundary quality; (iii) no self-containedness, no hook title; (iv) no modality ablation; (v) label quality measured against a small human test set with unknown annotator count; (vi) data not yet public.
