## 1) Task definition
- [F] "Livestream Highlight Detection": given a long recorded livestream (visual V + audio A), output a set of non-overlapping proposals {(s_i, e_i)} with s_i < e_i < s_{i+1}; each proposal should cover a highlight "topic or discussion". Model deliberately uses speech only ("less necessary information in shot/scene changes than in streamers' language").
- [F] Input: ASR transcript split into utterances (avg 298.58 utterances/stream, 24.63 words/utterance), speaker label per utterance, mel-spectrum chunk per utterance. Streams avg ~47 min (2,861.55 h / 3,655 records [I computed]). Output: unconstrained number of proposals; GT averages 9.60 highlights/stream, 211.87 s each, 15.04 utterances, covering 7.65% of the stream.

## 2) Label source and construction
- [F] AntHighlight: 3,655 Chinese livestream recordings from an Alipay-supported platform (funds/insurance/wealth theme), 2,861.55 h; 10 human annotators watched each whole stream and marked start/end boundaries. Definition: highlights are "valuable segments with an integral topic and complete content" (product introductions, industry profiles, investment suggestions), plus meaningful viewer-streamer interactions (Q&A, experience sharing). Split 3,055/100/500.
- [F] No inter-annotator agreement, annotation guideline details, or pilot statistics are reported. [I] Labels are editorial judgments of topic completeness, not audience behaviour; one narrow commercial domain with scripted-ish sales talk.

## 3) How start/end are determined
- [F] Candidate cut points = utterance boundaries (the model labels each utterance as start/end/in/out). Network (Pivot Transformer = Bi-GRU init + block-wise multi-head attention with pivot tokens, external/internal gates, shifted-window copy to remove absolute-position effects) outputs per-utterance highlight confidence h_i and boundary probability b_i; loss = boundary BCE + lambda * highlight BCE.
- [F] Inference is a dynamic program over four states (start, end, in, out) that maximises the accumulated score of a legal state sequence (a finite-state automaton), compared with a threshold-only "Simple" decoder and a "Greedy" start-end pairing decoder.
- [F] Boundary-specific metric: Boundary F1 where a predicted start (or end) counts as correct if within 5 s of a GT boundary, ignoring GT segments shorter than 10 s; reported separately for start and end, plus AP at tIoU 0.5-0.9.

## 4) Self-containedness / context completeness
- [F] Present only through the annotation definition ("integral topic", "complete content") and, implicitly, boundary supervision at utterance level; the model sees global context through pivots. No explicit completeness metric, no dangling-reference test.

## 6) Modality ablation
- [F] Table 2 only: semantic text alone is the base; adding speaker embeddings (+0.6 AP@0.5, +1.0 F1-start, +1.7 F1-end... S 42.2/45.1 -> S+K 43.2/46.8) helps more than adding mel "pattern" embeddings (S+P 43.3/46.2 vs S 42.2/45.1 in F1; AP almost unchanged); all three together best. Authors conclude speaker identification is more necessary than the prosody-like embedding. No laughter/music/visual modality is tested; the audio feature is a downsampled mel-spectrum per utterance, not an event detector.

## 9) Difference from our direction
- Closest precedent for treating clip boundaries as structured prediction over speech-unit boundaries with a boundary-aware metric and a global DP decoder over start/end/in/out states -- directly reusable idea for our "candidate cut points" formulation (RQ3).
- Differences: (i) segments are topic-level (~3.5 min), not 15-90 s short-form clips; (ii) labels are human topic-completeness judgments on a single sales-talk domain, with no viewer behaviour and no engagement or hook notion; (iii) no ranking of K clips for value, no title; (iv) no explicit self-containedness criteria (only the "integral topic" guideline); (v) no external baselines or LLMs; (vi) audio treated as mel summary; (vii) the dataset is Chinese finance livestream, limited transfer to podcast/interview content.
