# D20 Delving Deep into Engagement Prediction of Short Videos (SnapUGC)
Li, Li, Lu, Li, Ma, Krishnan, Wang (Dasong Li, Wenjie Li, Baili Lu, Hongsheng Li, Sizhuo Ma, Gurunandan Krishnan, Jian Wang; CUHK MMLab and Snap Inc.). arXiv 2410.00289v1 (30 Sep 2024); ECCV 2024 [F: DOI 10.1007/978-3-031-72949-2_17 via OpenAlex; GitHub README cites the ECVA ECCV 2024 paper page].
Reading depth: full main text (Sec 1-5) from arXiv HTML read via extraction and re-checked against the GitHub README; supplementary (cross-platform results) NOT available [V].

## 1. Task definition [F]
Predict, from video content alone, two aggregated engagement labels for a newly published short video: NAWP and ECR. Formalised as (NAWP-hat, ECR-hat) = G(v | R), conditional on the platform recommender R (Eq. 3, Sec 4.1). Framed as cold-start prediction independent of user/creator/context (Sec 1).

## 2. Label source and construction [F]
- Source: Snapchat Spotlight; "aggregated engagement data derived from viewing statistics". ~90,000 videos, 10-60 s (Sec 3.2); 90/10 train/test (Sec 5.1); no validation split described.
- Filter: only videos with view number >2,000 (Sec 3.2) "to mitigate sampling bias from small view counts"; Intro says metrics come from >2,000 viewers. Whether views = viewers is not clarified. Recommender identity, exploration policy and time window are not given.
- AWT = mean watch time; AWP = AWT/duration (can exceed 1 with rewatch). NAWP: fit the top 3% of AWT against duration with a line f_max(d)=0.556 d + 5.64 (Eq. 1, Fig. 2b/c; fitting procedure/binning not stated), f_min=0, NAWP=min((AWT-f_min)/(f_max-f_min),1) (Eq. 2); top-3% videos assigned 1 by clipping. ECR = fraction of viewers watching beyond 5 s (wording "at least 5 s" vs "exceeding 5 s" inconsistent). NAWP-ECR correlation 0.926; both are bimodal (swipe-away) (Fig. 2f-h).
- Privacy: only aggregated metrics, no viewer histories (Sec 1, 4.1).

## 3. Bias sources and treatment [F]
- Duration: AWT/AWP decline with duration; handled by NAWP normalisation; ECR is nearly duration-independent. Feeding duration as an input did not help NAWP (0.696 -> 0.689 SRCC, Tab. 4) but helped AWP/AWT (0.665->0.681; 0.668->0.683).
- Exposure/recommender: acknowledged explicitly; view counts "heavily influenced by recommendation systems and biased toward well-known creators" -> views not used as target; conditioning on R in notation only; R is neither modelled nor varied.
- Creator, follower count, upload time, title/thumbnail effects: creator information is excluded by design; upload time and thumbnail not addressed; title/description treated as a legitimate input feature.
- Popularity-selection: the >2,000-view filter makes the evaluated population non-cold-start (my inference [I]); not discussed.
- MOS annotator bias is the foil: VQA models trained on MOS correlate weakly with AWT (Tab. 1: UVQ 0.084/0.156/0.290/0.289 and DOVER 0.073/0.148/0.305/0.286 SRCC at durations ~20/30/40/50 s).

## 4. Assumptions of the label normalisation [F]
Maximum AWT is linear in duration over 10-60 s; videos at the 97th percentile AWT are "equally most popular" regardless of duration; f_min=0. No goodness-of-fit reported; supplement claims the linear form transfers to Kuaishou/YouTube data <=60 s (not verified here [V]). Not an IPS/propensity method.

## 5. Metrics and numbers [F]
SRCC, PLCC, RMSE for NAWP and ECR, plus RMSE on the top 10% by NAWP (Eq. 6). Tab. 3 (NAWP SRCC/PLCC/RMSE/top10-RMSE; ECR SRCC/PLCC/RMSE/top10-RMSE): Ours 0.696/0.701/0.172/0.181; 0.675/0.688/0.174/0.152. DOVER 0.635/0.636/0.206/0.216; 0.619/0.622/0.203/0.189. Ours-VQA 0.625/0.632/0.188/0.191; 0.605/0.620/0.189/0.171. VSFA 0.609/0.615/0.192/0.199; 0.576/0.591/0.197/0.174. PVQ 0.590; MD-VQA 0.606; FastVQA 0.587 NAWP SRCC. Joint NAWP+ECR training beats separate (Tab. 5; column labels appear transposed vs Tab. 3, so I use Tab. 3 values) [F with caveat]. Training: batch 8, 70k iterations, Adam, cosine LR 1e-4 -> 1e-7, joint L2 losses.

## 6. Modality/feature ablation [F] (incremental, NAWP SRCC, Fig. 3 text)
VQA features (EfficientNet semantic + UVQ-style distortion + ResNet-3D action) 0.625 -> + YAMNet top-5 sound labels (audio; as text) 0.636 -> + title and description (T5) 0.651 -> + mPLUG-2 captions 0.657 -> + mPLUG-2 mid-layer visual features 0.689 -> + DOVER aesthetic features 0.696; + ASR transcripts: no gain (only ~30% of videos have useful transcripts); + visual emotion (WSCNet) 0.690 (dropped). Cumulative additions, not leave-one-out; no separate audio-only/text-only models; no ablation for ECR. No comments/danmaku/uploader features exist in this dataset.

## 8. Self-stated limitations [F/I]
No limitations section. Concedes: transcripts and visual emotion did not help; DOVER better SRCC but worse RMSE. Not discussed: recommender dependence beyond notation, creator effects, thumbnails, geographic/demographic coverage, effect of the 2,000-view filter, bimodality for regression.

## 9. Difference from our direction [I]
Whole-video retention of 10-60 s UGC from a short-video feed, labelled by an algorithmic feed, versus our clip selection inside a 30 min-hours speech-first video with replay/clip-view weak labels. Useful: (i) ECR (P(watch>5 s)) is a direct quantification of hook strength; (ii) duration normalisation recipe (NAWP) and the finding that explicit duration input can hurt; (iii) evidence that content alone yields SRCC~0.70 with no creator information; (iv) negative evidence for transcripts on short noisy UGC. No clip boundaries, no self-containedness, no debiasing of exposure.
