# D01 Joachims, Swaminathan, Schnabel - Unbiased Learning-to-Rank with Biased Feedback
arXiv 1608.04468 v1 (16 Aug 2016; text copy marked "Under review"); published at WSDM 2017 (DOI 10.1145/3018661.3018699 via OpenAlex [F for DOI, venue per OpenAlex]).
Reading depth: sections 1, 3, 4, 5.1-5.4, 6, 7.1-7.6, 8-9 read from PDF text (pdftotext on harness-cached copy). Related work (sec 2) skimmed. Formal proofs of sec 4 followed only for the IPS unbiasedness step.

## 1. Task definition [F]
Learn a ranking function from observational implicit feedback (clicks) logged under a deployed ranker, without explicit relevance labels. Performance measure used for derivation: sum of ranks of relevant results, Delta(y|x,r)=sum rank(y|ranking)*r(y) (Eq. 2); extensible to other additive measures.

## 2. Label source and construction [F]
Labels are clicks c_i(y) in {0,1}. Under the position-based propensity model (sec 5.1) a click is generated iff the result is examined (o_i(y)=1, probability p_rank depends only on rank in the presented ranking) and relevant. Noise model (5.2): P(click|relevant,examined)=eps+, P(click|irrelevant,examined)=eps-, 1>=eps+>eps->=0. Synthetic study: Yahoo LTR Challenge set 1, relevance binarised (ratings 3-4 relevant), presented ranking from a weak "production ranker" (Ranking SVM trained on 1% of data), p_rank=(1/rank)^eta with eta=1, eps-=0.1, eps+=1 (about 33% noisy clicks; rank-10 examined 10%). Live study: scientific-paper full-text search engine (arXiv), 1000-dim query-doc features, 16-day train (5,437 click events) and 5-day validation (1,755 clicks).

## 3. Bias sources and treatment [F]
Only presentation (position) bias is modelled explicitly. Trust bias and saliency bias are named as extensions (5.4): trust bias by conditioning the click probability on rank; saliency by letting p_r be a regression function instead of a rank-only vector. Selection of items (what the production ranker shows) enters through propensities; no duration, channel or content-appeal effects.

## 4. Assumptions and applicability [F]
- Counterfactual model: only part of the relevance vector is observed (o_i). IPS estimate of the loss is unbiased for any new ranking iff Q(o_i(y)=1|x_i, y-bar_i, r_i)>0 for every y that is relevant (irrelevant items need not have positive propensity) (sec 4, proof via linearity of expectation). Only clicked (observed and relevant) items contribute, so no negatives needed.
- Position-based model: unbiased when p_r>0 for all ranks (Eq. 5). Examination depends only on rank in the shown list, not on relevance or content.
- Propensities known or estimated; only needed up to a positive multiplicative constant (does not change the ordering of systems). Estimation: swap intervention with landmark rank k: swap rank-k and rank-r docs, CTR ratio proportional to p_r/p_k (5.3); lighter than uniform randomisation.
- Click noise: IPS risk is then biased wrt true relevance but order-preserving, so the ERM minimiser is unchanged (5.2, derivation in text).
- Variance, not bias, is the cost: more data helps Propensity SVM-Rank but not the naive learner (7.3: "5x" data experiment); a clipped-propensity variant is shown in Fig. 1.
- Learning method: Propensity SVM-Rank (sec 6): SVM-Rank with each clicked example weighted by 1/q_j; candidate set typically a few hundred docs from a stage-one ranker; linear scoring function.
- Click models other than the position model (cascade etc.) can serve as propensity models if marginal click probabilities of clicked docs can be computed in hindsight (5.4). Explicit-rating case: propensities from self-selection, observation pattern fully known.

## 5. Evaluation and key numbers [F]
Synthetic (7.1-7.5): Propensity SVM-Rank beats Naive SVM-Rank whenever substantial bias exists (Fig. 2); robust to noise levels eps- up to 0.3 (59.8% noisy clicks; Fig. 3) and to misspecified eta (Fig. 4; true eta=1; n=170K clicks, eps-=0.1). Metric: average rank of relevant results; baseline production ranker S0; skyline = noise-free full-information Ranking SVM. Numeric points are in figures only; I did not extract values [V].
Live (7.6): per-query balanced interleaving. Propensity SVM-Rank vs production ranker: 87 wins / 48 losses / 83 ties (two-tailed binomial sign test p=0.001, relative risk 0.71). Propensity SVM-Rank vs Naive SVM-Rank: 95 / 60 / 102 (p=0.006, RR 0.77). Propensity estimation in the live run: 7 days of swap interventions with landmark k=1, top doc swapped with a uniformly random rank in 1..21.

## 8. Self-stated limitations / future work [F]
Sec 9: need better propensity models; other learning methods (pointwise, listwise) should be adapted to propensity-weighted ERM (conjectured, not shown); propensity-weighted ERM could also remove pooling bias in test collections. Position model called "arguably one of the simplest"; the experiments cover only a linear SVM scorer.

## 9. Difference from our direction [I]
(1) There is a well-defined presentation list with ranks and a logging policy; our replay heatmap is an aggregate over viewers with no per-impression log, so the propensity (probability a viewer reaches or replays a given second) is neither logged nor interventionally estimable. (2) Click = examined AND relevant; replay = reached AND chosen to re-watch; the second factor is also content-dependent in ways the model treats as "relevance", but seek-back, chapters and intro effects break "examination depends only on rank". (3) The method needs relevance-positive propensity; at late timestamps reach probability is small, so weights explode (variance). (4) It debiases for ranking quality of documents, not for self-containedness or boundaries. (5) What transfers: the theory of what is needed (propensity for the relevant items, scale-free), order-preserving noise argument, swap-style estimation, and the idea of reranking within a candidate set (maps to candidate clip proposals).
