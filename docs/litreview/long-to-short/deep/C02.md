# C02 TREC 2021 Podcasts Track Overview
Karlgren, Jones, Carterette, Clifton, Eskevich, Jones, Reddy, Tanaka, Tanveer. TREC 2021 proceedings (NIST SP 500-335); PDF https://trec.nist.gov/pubs/trec30/papers/Overview-Pod.pdf (converted with pdftotext in scratchpad); task page https://trecpodcasts.github.io/. reading_depth = full-text (12-page overview; per-run tables partly garbled by extraction).

## 1) Task definition
- [F] Task 1 segment retrieval: topical retrieval of fixed two-minute segments (start on the minute, 1-min overlap, ~3.4M segments, 340 +/- 70 words each) plus three reranked lists of the same relevant segments by non-topical criteria: Entertaining (speakers intend to be amusing rather than informative), Subjective (speaker explicitly expresses a polar opinion on the topic), Discussion (more than one speaker contributing non-trivially). 2021: 40 topical + 10 known-item topics (refinding merged into known-item); reranking not applied to known-item.
- [F] Task 2 summarization: (1) short text snippet of the episode content in grammatical utterances; (2) a new requirement: an audio file of up to one minute selected from the podcast "to give the user a sense of what the podcast sounds like". Task framing: help the user decide whether to listen.
- [F] The overview states the tasks were "elaborated to encourage participants to use audio".
## 2) Labels / annotation source and agreement
- [F] NIST assessors judged submitted segments with access to both the automatic transcript (including text before and after the segment as context) and the audio, on PEGFB; reranking criteria judged for relevant segments. Summaries: 193 episodes assessed on EGFB plus the 8 boolean attributes; audio clips got a binary "conveys the sound and feel of the podcast" rating. No inter-assessor agreement reported.
- [F] Assessed relevant segments over 2021 topics: P/E/G/F counts 113/257/556/370 (Table 4 row order as extracted; ~34 PEGF per query); entertaining 457, subjective 1,081, discussion 729 segments over the topical queries (Table 5).
## 3) Context / self-containedness embedded in the grading
- [F] The PEGFB descriptors are partly about entry points: Excellent = "ideal entry point for a human listener", e.g., a segment that begins at or near the start of a discussion "immediately signalling relevance and context"; Fair = "sub-par entry point", including segments that "start well into a discussion without providing enough context for understanding" or switch from non-relevant to relevant. So limited context-sufficiency is penalised implicitly inside a relevance grade, without a separate scale or measurement. Summary attribute 8 "sentence" asks whether the start and end of the summary are good sentence/paragraph start and end points.
## 4) Unit
- [F] Fixed 2-minute grid for retrieval (overlap to avoid splitting sentences); text summaries unbounded length; audio clip up to 60 s selected by the participant (no boundary evaluation beyond acceptability binary).
## 5) Metrics and key numbers
- [F] Retrieval: mean nDCG (ideal ranking of all relevant segments), nDCG@30, P@10; overlapping segments from the same episode treated as independent. Table 7 topical (QR): best runs nDCG about 0.51-0.53 (tp_mt5, TUW hybrid/tasb, osc vec_tok), nDCG@30 up to ~0.37, P@10 up to ~0.41; UCL audio 1 nDCG 0.44 / audio 2 0.34 (garbled extraction, treat as approximate [V]). Reranked lists Tables 8-10: entertaining best nDCG about 0.30-0.31, nDCG@30 up to 0.20, P@10 up to 0.18; subjective best nDCG ~0.47-0.49; discussion best ~0.43-0.44.
- [F] Summarization (Table 12, quality 0-4 / audio acceptability 0-1): PoliTO 50_64-128 (abstractive) 1.06 / 0.98; Unicamp1 1.04 / 1.00; PoliTO 25_32-128 1.03 / 0.98; Unicamp2 1.01 / 0.50; PoliTO 100_32-128 0.98 / 0.99; PoliTO 50_32-128 0.91 / 0.99; baseline first-minute transcript 0.81 / 0.96; Hotspot1 (extractive; speech emotion recognition for the clip) 0.43 / 0.95; theTuringTest1 0.34 / 0.20; Webis extractive 0.26 / 0.92, abstractive 0.23 / 0.94; theTuringTest2 0.18 / 0.21. Quality scores much lower than 2020 (best >2.0 then); audio acceptability mostly high; audio score only weakly correlated with summary quality; topics attribute most correlated with quality.
## 6) Modality ablation
- [F] Audio-using runs exist (UCL audio reranking with YAMNet-based classifier/rules on top of BM25; PoliTO fusing OpenSMILE audio features with SBERT sentence embeddings for extractive filtering; Hotspot1 speech-emotion-based clip selection), but the overview reports no with/without-audio ablation, and the audio reranking runs do not appear among the top entries for the reranked lists in the (partly garbled) tables. So the track provides no quantified audio gain [F absence in the overview; I on ranking].
## 7) Data / code / license
- [F] Dataset required a data sharing agreement (191 signed in 2021; 377 downloaded transcripts, 26 downloaded audio); evaluation script and qrels on the NIST 2021 data page; dataset terms not read in detail [V].
## 8) Limitations stated
- Summarization quality scores lower than 2020 and the discrepancy is "an open question" (test set, methods or assessors).
## 9) Differences from our direction
- Query-driven retrieval and episode-level description rather than K standalone clips; fixed 2-min units; no engagement labels; context-sufficiency only implicit in the grade text. Closest transferable ideas: assessors judging with transcript plus audio plus surrounding context; "entry point" language; reranking criteria for entertaining/subjective/discussion segments (non-topical value dimensions).
