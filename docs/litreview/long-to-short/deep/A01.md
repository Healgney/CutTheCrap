# A01 Repurpose-10K (arXiv 2412.08879v2; AAAI 2025) -- deep read

Read: arXiv HTML v2 in full (abstract, intro, related work, dataset overview/collection/analysis/human evaluation, framework, setting, results, ablations, applications, conclusion, references) + GitHub repo (README, LICENSE metadata, data/train|val|test.json which I parsed myself with perl). Sources: https://arxiv.org/html/2412.08879v2 ; https://github.com/yongliang-wu/Repurpose . The paper has no limitations or ethics section.

## 1) Task definition
- [F] "Long-to-short video repurposing": input = a user-generated video, "typically longer than 30 minutes"; output = multiple clips "around 60 s", each a coherent, engaging, self-contained sub-topic. Query-free; treated as temporal grounding without a query.
- [F] Input representation: 1 fps, 1-s segments; CLIP ViT-B/32 (512-d), PANNs per second (2048-d), WhisperX ASR sentences -> all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (384-d), sentences overlapping several segments are duplicated, empty windows get an [empty] token.
- [F] Output: per-segment selection probability + regression of clip extent (1-D IoU loss, ReLU regression head); inference: threshold 0.5, soft-NMS, top-k with k = "3 clips every ten minutes on average" from training statistics.
- [F, computed from released JSON] The data do not match this k: train/val/test JSON contain 120,942 clips over 11,211 samples = 10.8 clips per sample and 5.3 clips per 10 minutes; selected clips cover on average 29-30% of a sample (median ~23-25%).

## 2) Label source and construction (who, how aligned, scale, bias)
- [F] Platform: OPUS (opus.pro); authors include Opus AI Research. Pipeline: (1) the platform's LLM-based tool topic-segments a YouTube video into coarse-cut sections; (2) users mark sections "like"/"post"; (3) creators manually refine start/end for the clips they keep. Only re-edited, endorsed clips are kept -> 120,925 clips, 8,398 videos from 114,883 creators. Videos >30 min are split -> 11,210 samples; reported total 4,539.94 h; mean 0.54 h per original video; mean 18.9M views.
- [F] Human validation (Table 2): 110 videos -> 462 user clips (mean 58.66 s) vs 462 random 30-90 s clips vs 3 clips/video from two professional creators; 10 participants rated Engagement and Completeness (1-5): random 1.508/1.623; professional 3.938/3.738; user labels 3.868/3.418.
- [F, computed] My parse of the released annotations: 8,968/1,121/1,122 samples in train/val/test; 8,399 unique YouTube IDs; clip duration mean 39.5 s, median 36-37 s, 5th/95th percentile 16.6/76 s, 98% of clips inside 15-90 s, <0.5% longer than 90 s; sample (chunk) length mean ~1,220 s, max exactly 1,800 s (chunks of <=30 min); mean gap between consecutive clips 65 s; 7.5% of consecutive clip pairs are separated by <1 s (back-to-back clips). Sum of timeRange lengths is 3,803 h, not the 4,539.94 h in the paper [V: unexplained; maybe unannotated tails dropped].
- [F, computed] Split leakage risk: 807 YouTube IDs occur in more than one split (404 in both train and test, 404 in train and val, 63 in val and test), i.e. different 30-min chunks of the same video fall in different splits (split is by chunk, not by video).
- [F] Paper's "median ~60 s" statement vs data: clip median is ~36 s (see above) -- the "around 60 s" appears to describe the human-study sample rather than the dataset.
- [I] Bias: candidates are proposals from OpusClip's own segmentation, so label = P(user accepts tool proposal and re-trims) rather than P(clip is best in video); creator channel/popularity skew (videos averaging 18.9M views); users self-select videos worth repurposing; no analysis of genre/podcast share; "like/post" intent (posting) conflates engagement with the user's brand fit. Paper does not discuss these.

## 3) How start/end are determined
- [F] Ground truth: creator's manual trimming on top of the tool's coarse cut; no snapping rule to sentence, speaker turn or shot is described.
- [F] Model: joint per-segment classification + boundary regression (IoU loss) + soft-NMS; no candidate-cut list; evaluation is tIoU only. No boundary-specific metric (no start/end error in seconds, no sentence-boundary match).
- [I] The 1-s granularity plus tIoU makes starts/ends at +-a few seconds nearly indistinguishable from mid-sentence cuts; the paper never checks that.

## 4) Self-containedness / context completeness
- [F] Only as a design statement: clips are "self-contained sub-topics" with "a complete narrative", "begin with intriguing content and end with fitting remarks" (qualitative Fig. 4); related work argues highlight datasets "do not require high integrity" of the content and hence are unsuitable. Measurement: only the human "Completeness" 1-5 rating of annotation sources (label quality), not used as a training signal, not applied to model outputs. Captions (ASR) are used only as an auxiliary cue for aligning audio and visual attention.
- Metric for model outputs: none.

## 5) Evaluation and key numbers
- [F] tIoU at {0.5,...,0.9} and Avg (Table 3): SL-Module 9.87; Moment-DETR(V) 10.13; QD-DETR(V) 10.01; UMT(V&A) 9.31; QD-DETR(V&A) 10.16; Ours(V&A&C) 11.57 with 24.94/16.35/10.14/4.86/1.57 at 0.5/0.6/0.7/0.8/0.9. Baselines apparently trained on this data (text says all models compared; training protocol for baselines not detailed [V]).
- [F] Ablation Table 4 (Avg tIoU; UF = uni-modal focal loss, AL = alignment loss): A 9.66; V 9.52; C 9.59; A&V 10.31; A&V+UF 10.71; A&V+UF+AL 10.96; A&V&C 10.75; +UF 11.28; full 11.57 (all three losses). Layer config Ns/Nc/Nf: 1/4/4 10.48; 3/3/3 11.57; 5/2/2 9.48; 7/1/1 8.57.
- [F] No LLM/MLLM baseline, no human evaluation of model outputs, no engagement metric.

## 6) Modality ablation
- [F] Audio-only 9.66 > caption-only 9.59 > visual-only 9.52 (differences ~0.1); A&V 10.31 (+0.65 over best single); adding captions to A&V +0.44 (10.75) before extra losses; the final gain of full model over A&V+UF+AL is +0.61. Features are generic (CLIP/PANNs/MiniLM), so "audio" is AudioSet classification embeddings, not laughter/prosody. All effects are tiny in absolute terms (avg tIoU ~10-11).

## 7) Data / code / licence
- [F] Code: github.com/yongliang-wu/Repurpose, GitHub API licence MIT; data/ holds train.json 5.3 MB, val.json 0.66 MB, test.json 0.66 MB (fields: youtube_id, timeRange, segments, timeRangeOffset, segmentsOffset, coverage); README: download source videos with yt-dlp, extract features with video_features/panns_inference/sentence-transformers/whisperX. Annotations are in the repo; no features/videos/captions released.

## 8) Self-stated limitations
- [F] None section. Only remarks: untrained annotators are unreliable (motivation), model is a baseline; results are low in absolute terms.

## 9) Difference from our direction
- Closest in task framing (long UGC -> clips, behaviour-derived labels) but: (i) outputs have no hook title; (ii) label is accept/re-trim of one tool's proposals, so it cannot supply negatives for the whole video or an engagement ranking among K clips; (iii) boundaries are untouched by linguistic structure and evaluated by plain tIoU (avg 11.57 means no model reaches reliable agreement) -- a sentence/turn-snapped candidate formulation with boundary metrics is open; (iv) self-containedness is a qualitative claim + 1-5 rating, not a defined criterion or classifier; (v) generic features and no LLM/MLLM baselines; (vi) possible split leakage and mismatch between stated (~60 s, k=3/10 min) and released (36 s median, 5.3/10 min) statistics should be accounted for if we reuse it as a benchmark; (vii) no podcast/interview breakdown reported.
