# C15 Bridging Context Gaps: Enhancing Comprehension in Long-Form Social Conversations Through Contextualized Excerpts
Mohanty, Xuan, Jobraeel, Kumar, Roy, Kabbara. COLING 2025; arXiv 2412.19966 (v1, 28 Dec 2024). Read: all body sections incl. Tables 1-4, Limitations, Ethics (HTML); survey wording lives in image-only appendix figures (not readable). reading_depth = sections:1-9 (appendix F screenshots unread).

## 1) Task definition
- [F] Given an excerpt e from a long recorded multi-party conversation C, produce a context-enriched excerpt e~ = f(C, e) (f = LLM) so that a reader outside the conversation understands it better (Section 4). Context factors: speaker characteristics, speaker motivation, relevant locations/events (drawing on Hovy and Yang 2021). Two prompts: implicit (CEE_i, zero-shot) and explicit (CEE_e, in-context examples asking for the attributes), plus an extrinsic term-clarification step.
- [F] Secondary use (Section 7): summaries generated from full conversation vs excerpt-only vs context-enriched excerpts.

## 2) Labels / annotation source and agreement
- [F] Source conversations: Fora corpus (Schroeder et al. 2024), 262 facilitated conversations, avg 66 min, median 6 participants, avg 152 turns.
- [F] HSE: 1,774 human-highlighted salient excerpts over 177 conversations, mean 111 words (Section 3; Section 6.1 reports 128 words for the 90 evaluated excerpts), >75% with topic tags. The text does not say who highlighted them, what instructions they got, or how excerpt boundaries (sentence/turn/free span) were set.
- [F] Attribute annotation: Prolific crowd, 35 conversations / 100 excerpts, about 90 kept after removing inattentive responses (by completion time), 4-5 annotators each, then a "secondary review" for consistency set the ground truth (procedure not described).
- [F] Inter-annotator agreement: none reported for attribute annotation or for the Likert ratings.
- [F] Evaluation raters: 75 Prolific raters (99-100% approval), at least 3 per excerpt; 433 responses in the CEE_i vs CEE_e comparison; 30 raters in the LLM comparison; summary evaluation 34 evaluators / 156 responses.

## 3) How out-of-context understandability is defined and measured
- [F] No formal definition of "context gap" or of understandability. It is operationalized as 1-5 Likert ratings of understandability, readability, low redundancy, completeness, cohesiveness (plus speaker-perception items). Exact survey wording is only in screenshot figures (App. F.1), unreadable here [V].
- [F] The "original excerpt" ratings are the measurement of what is lost: Table 1 (433 responses): understandability 3.43, readability 3.40, low redundancy 3.24, completeness 3.07, cohesiveness 3.23 for the raw excerpt, versus CEE_i 4.11 / 3.90 / 3.53 / 3.96 / 3.93 and CEE_e 4.13 / 4.10 / 3.75 / 3.83 / 4.03 (all significantly above the original by Welch t-test; CEE_e > CEE_i on readability, redundancy, completeness-lower).
- [F] Statistics: Welch t-tests; no t values, df, effect sizes or multiple-comparison correction; Section 6.1 says p<0.05 but the Table 2 caption uses p<0.1 (inconsistency).
- [F] Automatic proxy: only a faithfulness F1 (GPT-4o extraction + SBERT cosine vs HSE attribute annotations), not a comprehension metric; no correlation between faithfulness and human understandability is reported.
- [F] Which part of the gap is anaphora vs background: not analysed. 128 rater responses flagged terms needing elaboration (mostly locations, abbreviations, specialised terms, participant names); CEE_e addressed 55 of them.

## 4) Start/end/unit
- [F] The unit is a human-highlighted excerpt; boundaries are given, not predicted. Whether excerpts span multiple speakers is not stated; Appendix B examples appear single-speaker [I].

## 5) Metrics and key numbers
- [F] Table 1 as above. Length: original ~128 words, CEE_i ~267, CEE_e ~55 (Section 6.1).
- [F] Table 2 (explicit contexts by LLM, 30 raters): understandability GPT 3.70, Claude 3.81, Llama 3.58; completeness 3.67 / 3.72 / 3.58; cohesiveness 3.66 / 3.81* / 3.47*. Llama contexts ~538 words, GPT-4o ~296, Claude ~264.

## 8) Limitations stated
- LLM output variability (temperature 0 as partial mitigation); open-ended attributes give low F1 because annotators differ; need for better faithfulness metrics (NER and entailment judged unsuitable); only 90 excerpts analysed because multi-annotator ground truth is costly.

## 9) Differences from our direction
- Repairs excerpts by adding generated context instead of selecting excerpts that need none; our setting cannot add text to a clip (we choose start/end).
- Excerpts are human-chosen salient text passages (~110-130 words), not 15-90 s audio/video clips; no boundary choice, no ranking, no engagement signal, no hook.
- No automatic self-containedness score; no agreement numbers; small evaluation (90 excerpts).
- Useful as: evidence that raw salient excerpts of long conversations score only ~3.1-3.4/5 on understandability/completeness/cohesiveness, and as a template for a Likert rubric and a rater pool (Prolific, >=3 raters).
