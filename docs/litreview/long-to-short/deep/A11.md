## 1) Task definition
- [F] Authoring system that turns a long-form video (formative videos average 12-18 min; evaluation videos 5-20 min with visually diverse content, explicitly excluding podcasts/talks/lectures) into a short-form video of about one minute. Two operations: abstractive (LLM writes a short-form script, speech is synthesised with ElevenLabs, long-form clips are matched to the narration) and extractive (clips whose original speech is kept), combined by a replacement scoring step. Not a K-clip ranking problem; output is one composed video that the user refines.

## 2) Label source and construction
- [F] No labels or training. Evidence comes from (a) interviews + 40-min editing task with 5 professional editors (avg 11.6 years' experience; recruited via Upwork) and (b) an analysis of 20 pairs of YouTube long-form videos (mean 17.9 min, SD 7.9) and the creators' own YouTube Shorts (mean 47.5 s, SD 19.2): 13 of 20 shorts are "highlights" (one engaging/memorable moment), 7 of 20 are "summaries" (multiple clips); 18/20 link to the full video; 15/20 reuse visuals from the long version; 13/20 add new audio (mainly new narration); 13/20 have captions. These counts are descriptive; no dataset is released [V].
- [I] Observed fact useful for us: in this small natural sample, official shorts built from a long video are usually either a single moment or a re-narrated summary, and re-narration is common, so exact "official clip <-> source segment" alignment by audio would often fail (13/20 add new audio).

## 3) How start/end are determined
- [F] Extractive clips come from sentence segmentation of the transcript "similar to ROPE" (Wang et al. 2022), i.e. sentence-level candidate units; a combinatorial optimisation over sentence scores/lengths chooses spans; clips' timestamps are the selected sentences. Abstractive clips are matched to generated narration by a weighted score of visual connection (CLIP similarity between a segment's speech and its keyframe), noun-phrase coverage, and position-based alignment; speech playback speed is adjusted to fit. Permutations between abstractive and extractive options are ranked by GPT-3 loss ("coherence score"). No boundary-accuracy evaluation.

## 4) Self-containedness / context completeness
- [F] Coherence is addressed by (i) ordering/replacement via a language-model loss, (ii) editors' guidance in the formative study ("message needs to be clear", each new clip should be coherent with the story so far), (iii) the evaluation reports that missing hooks or conclusions are the most visible errors of the extractive baseline (V1: hook and conclusion missing; V5: hook missing). There is no per-clip self-containedness measure.
- [F] The paper characterises Opus and CapCut as extracting "a single highlight clip" and PodReels/ROPE as audio-based extraction of coherent narration; it positions itself as beyond audio-bound extraction.

## 5) Evaluation and key numbers
- [F] Results evaluation (Sec 5): 6 YouTube trending videos (5-20 min, visually diverse), 12 annotators ranked three methods (ROPE-style extractive baseline adapted with GPT-4o; Lotus abstractive; Lotus mixed): mean rank mixed 1.88 (SD 0.72), abstractive 2.06 (0.77), extractive 2.04 (0.82); difference not significant; preferences depend on the video (extractive/mixed preferred when the narrator talks to camera, abstractive when not). Also hook, narrative and visual appeal on 5-point Likert (Figure 9, values not extracted).
- [F] User study (Sec 6): 8 participants (avg 3.71 years of editing) with two ~14-15 min videos, 20 min per task, counterbalanced Lotus vs own tools; all participants said they would use Lotus; qualitative themes (generated result as a structure to start from; interactions logged in Table 1).
- [I] Small samples (6 videos, 12 raters, n=8); no significance; no engagement outcomes.

## 9) Difference from our direction
- Different goal: composes one short video with generated narration; scope includes abstractive generation, which we exclude; videos are visually diverse non-podcast content; evaluation is user preference on 6 videos. Contributes (a) a descriptive 20-pair analysis of real long->short pairs (single moment vs summary, high rate of new audio), (b) the claim that missing hook/conclusion are the visible failure modes of extractive baselines. Threat to novelty: low. Not suitable as a baseline, but supports adding hook/ending checks to our evaluation.
