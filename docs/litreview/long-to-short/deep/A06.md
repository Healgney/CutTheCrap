## 1) Task definition
- [F] Highlight detection for extremely long sports videos: input = full broadcast (>1 h, mean 2.00 h, 8 sports x 40 videos, total 640.18 h), query-free. Output = saliency score per 2-s clip; predictions are evaluated as ranked clips (HIT@1, mAP) and as a selected set (top-K with K = number of GT clips in that video; IoU). Baselines are given the pseudo-query "Highlight of this {video_type} video". GT highlight videos last ~3-30 min (Fig. 2), i.e. ~3-15% of the broadcast [I].

## 2) Label source and construction
- [F] Pairs (full broadcast, official highlight video) from official league/association YouTube channels, only videos with >10,000 views from neutral organisations, same broadcast source. Full videos manually trimmed to game footage (rules per sport, Appendix Table 11).
- [F] Alignment: downsample to 144p; for the middle frame of every 1-s highlight clip pick the full-video frame with highest PSNR (PSNR chosen over CLIP/ResNet because repeated plays/camera angles fool learned features); post-processing: prefer the frame 1 s after the previous match unless a candidate beats it by more than tau (tau=5; ablation tau=1/5/10 gives remaining rate 89.78/88.16/80.36% and f*/f+ ratios 72.3/51.7/27.4%); automatic filter drops pairs with PSNR<20 (8.3% of frames removed); two annotators perform a binary visual check of 16-pair grids, which changes 3.7% of the automatically aligned frames (95% of those restore false negatives; true errors 0.18% of frames, 99.82% precision of the automatic stage).
- [F] Alignment quality (Table 3): mean PSNR 26.74, SSIM 0.865, CLIP similarity 0.955. Label rule: 1-s window around each matched frame; each 2-s clip is positive if >=50% covered; max alignment error 0.5 s.
- [F] Label-validity user study: 10 raters, 120 clips (15/sport), Likert 1-5: positives 3.42, near negatives (<30 s from a highlight boundary) 1.94, far negatives 1.97. Per sport (positive/negative mean, Krippendorff alpha): soccer 4.00/1.40, 0.702; basketball 3.98/1.73, 0.654; American football 3.84/2.14, 0.530; ice hockey 3.36/1.92, 0.459; baseball 3.08/1.66, 0.644; racing alpha 0.22 and rugby low agreement (values partly extracted) -- domain knowledge needed.
- [F] Known biases/limits: broadcaster's highlight reel is an editorial choice (replays, curated by channel), not viewer behaviour; the near-vs-far negatives scoring identically (1.94 vs 1.97) means clip boundaries are not noisy; official highlight videos include frames not in the broadcast (logos, graphics) -> removed.

## 3) How start/end are determined
- [F] GT boundaries come from the contiguous matched ranges of the official highlight (by construction exact to ~0.5 s at 2-s resolution). Model side: TransNet V2 shots -> merge adjacent shots when an ASR sentence (words <1 s apart) spans the cut -> segments capped at 2 min; the LLM scores whole segments, and every 2-s clip inherits an overlap-weighted segment score. So predicted highlight boundaries are segment boundaries (shot cuts adjusted by sentence continuity) -- a candidate-cut formulation that avoids cutting mid-sentence by construction.
- [F] Boundary-related metric: window-based event F1 in Appendix D (consecutive highlight clips form events; predictions near a GT event within a window count); TF-Selector 27.38, second behind UMT; main metrics (HIT@K, IoU) are overlap-based, not boundary-error metrics.

## 4) Self-containedness / context completeness
- [F] Not about viewer comprehension. "Context-aware segments" means grouping shots that share one scene/sentence so the LLM scores a coherent unit and clips of the same scene get consistent scores. No metric of self-containedness.

## 6) Modality ablation (Table 10)
- [F] Caption only (C): mAP 10.54 / HIT@1 15.00 / HIT@K 12.54 / IoU 8.64. C + audio volume (A): 10.29/16.25/12.38/9.23. C + transcript (T): 12.18/21.25/16.21/11.11. C+T+A: 12.81/26.56/16.90/10.58. So transcript adds +6.25 HIT@1 and +3.67 HIT@K; audio volume alone adds ~+1.25 HIT@1 / -0.16 HIT@K; with transcript, audio adds +5.31 HIT@1 / +0.69 HIT@K but IoU -0.53. The 'audio' modality is raw volume (commentary excitement/crowd), not event detection. Single table, no variance or test-set size reported in the sections read [V].

## 8) Self-stated limitations
- [F] Pipeline requires paired full and highlight videos (limits extension beyond sports); preprocessing (alignment, captioning) is slow; training-free reliance on VLM/LLM may limit flexibility. Future: broader long-form domains, more robust reasoning.

## 9) Difference from our direction
- Direct evidence for the "official full video <-> official shorter cut" label pipeline at scale (PSNR matching works because the highlight is cut from the same broadcast) -- directly applicable to official podcast/talk-show clips only when frames are re-used verbatim (also true for most clip channels) but needs audio-based or text-based alignment when the clip is re-framed/vertically cropped [I].
- Differences: sports domain and editorial highlight-reel ground truth (not clips optimised for short-form platforms); unit is 2-s clip; no hook title, no K independent 15-90 s clips; no self-containedness or sentence-aligned boundary scoring (ASR used only to merge shots); audio = volume; zero-shot VTG baselines mis-specified for query-free task; the contribution to us is the label-construction recipe, HIT@K/IoU for variable-size GT sets, and the transcript-vs-volume ablation.
