# D21 VQualA 2025 Challenge on Engagement Prediction for Short Videos: Methods and Results (EVQA-SnapUGC track)
Organisers: Dasong Li, Sizhuo Ma, Hang Hua, Wenjie Li, Jian Wang, Chris Wei Zhou (+ 36 participant co-authors; 42 authors total). arXiv 2509.02969v1 (3 Sep 2025), ICCV 2025 Workshops (VQualA); DOI 10.1109/ICCVW69036.2025.00359 (via OpenAlex); CVF open-access page exists. Project page: github.com/dasongli1/SnapUGC_Engagement/tree/main/ECR_inference [F].
Reading depth: full text of the report (Sec 1-4, references skimmed, Appendix A affiliations) read locally from arXiv HTML. The report contains no ablations and no limitations section.

## 1. Task definition [F]
Predict Engagement Continuation Rate (ECR) = P(watch > 5 s) for short UGC videos from content (visual, audio) plus creator title/description. Motivation (Sec 1): cold-start recommendation; platforms show a new video to ~100 initial users and estimate latent popularity from those, which is noisy; predicting engagement from content could speed up cold-start. Not views, likes, shares. ECR chosen "for simplicity and clarity"; NAWP is the alternative.

## 2. Label source and construction [F]
SnapUGC (Sec 2.1): 120,651 videos, 5-60 s, from Snapchat Spotlight; only videos with >2000 views kept; train 106,192 / val 6,000 / test 8,459, each with creator title+description; "aggregated engagement data derived from viewing statistics" (>=1000 annotators-equivalent: Tab. 1 says ">= 1000 ... real user interactions"; text elsewhere says >2,000 users). Metrics (Sec 2.2): AWT, NAWP (assumption: top 3% AWT equally most popular; linear f_max, f_min=0), ECR (stable across durations, majority in [0,0.8]). ECR-NAWP correlation 0.928 (Fig. 2f); alpha/beta of NAWP "may vary across datasets or platforms", hence ECR chosen. For the challenge, "to protect creator privacy" the ECR used is derived from normalising the ranking of the real ECR [F]; exact transformation (rank/N?) not given [V].

## 3. Bias sources and treatment [F]
- Duration: ECR said to be more duration-independent; NAWP's linear parameters platform-dependent.
- View-count sampling bias: >2000 views filter only.
- Recommender/exposure, creator, upload time, thumbnail: not discussed in the report. Creator information is not provided to participants except title/description.
- Cold-start sampling bias is cited as a motivation for the *task*, not as a label problem.

## 4. Assumptions [I]
Rank-normalised ECR makes the evaluation purely rank-based (SROCC/PLCC on ranks), so calibration is irrelevant; labels remain conditioned on the Spotlight feed's exposure policy.

## 5. Evaluation and key numbers [F]
Metrics: SROCC, PLCC, "Final score" (not defined in the report; the winning team's paper D22 states 0.6*SROCC+0.4*PLCC; check 0.6*0.707+0.4*0.714=0.710). No model-size restriction ("to explore upper bound"). 97 participants, 15 valid test submissions; teams that submitted fact sheets are listed. Tab. 2 (Final/SROCC/PLCC): baseline 0.660/0.657/0.665 (Sec 3 text says baseline SROCC 0.660, PLCC 0.657: inconsistent with the table); 1 ECNU-SJTU VQA 0.710/0.707/0.714; 1* IMCL-DAMO 0.698/0.696/0.702; 3 HKUST-Cardiff-MI-BAAI 0.680/0.677/0.684 (visual only); 4 MCCE 0.667/0.666/0.668; 4* EasyVQA 0.667/0.664/0.671; 6 Rochester 0.449/0.405/0.515; 7 brucelyu17 0.441/0.439/0.444 (text only). Co-first place because the two top teams showed the largest gains over baseline. Text spells the second team "ICML-DAMO" once (typo). Tab. 2 lists ECNU-SJTU VQA's Video-LLaMA as "1.7B" while Sec 4.2 uses VideoLLaMA2.1-7B-AV (inconsistent) [F].

## 6. Modality use per team [F] (no ablations)
Baseline (Li et al.): per-frame semantic + distortion features, sound classification, author text, video captioning. ECNU-SJTU: VideoLLaMA2.1-7B-AV (first 8 or 5 frames + audio track + title/description, 'None' default; last-layer features -> MLP head: dropout, FC 2048, ReLU, FC 1) and Qwen2.5-VL-7B (8 frames + text, next-token regression); ensemble of 3 VideoLLaMA2 models (different seeds / frame counts) + 1 Qwen; 2xA800 / 8xA800, 1 epoch each; no ASR. IMCL-DAMO: baseline multi-modal branch + DOVER technical and aesthetic fragment branches + fully fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-7B; 4xA100, 48-72 h; baseline branch inference >12 h, Qwen branch ~6 h. HKUST-Cardiff-MI-BAAI: CLIP ViT quality predictor on sparse key frames + Laplacian-pyramid spatial rectifier + temporal rectifier; batch 32, 2 epochs, Adam 5e-5; visual only. MCCE: baseline + attention-weighted distortion fusion + perceptual encoder + global-local temporal aggregation, 3-5x training speedup. EasyVQA: CLIP on the first frame of each second of the first 5 s (matching the 5-s ECR definition), BERT title, BEATs audio, modality embeddings, Transformer encoder, MLP head. Rochester: frozen Skywork-VL-Reward-7B, 16 frames, regression head to [0,1], 4xH100 32 h. brucelyu17: Sentence-BERT (all-MiniLM-L6-v2) title/caption embeddings + background-music category embeddings -> XGBoost (grid: depth 3/6, lr 0.1/0.05/0.01, subsample 0.8/1.0).
Quantified modality statements: only the text-only entry (0.441 vs 0.710 for multimodal winner and 0.680 for a visual-only entry). Conclusion drawn by the authors: non-visual information "can also meaningfully contribute". No uploader/comment inputs.

## 9. Difference from our direction [I]
Challenge outcome measures prediction of first-5-s retention of whole short videos from a recommender feed, in rank space. It shows (i) content+title+audio models reach SROCC~0.71, with text-only ~0.44; (ii) multimodal LMMs are the best; (iii) nothing about long-video clip selection, boundaries, self-containedness, or debiasing. For our weak-label question it supplies a "hook" label (ECR) that is cleaner than views (duration-independent, viewer-level) but unavailable for clips cut from long YouTube videos.
